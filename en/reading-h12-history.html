The actual history list and error quantifiers

This appendix accompanies the main text, From actual search histories to current-group localization and far-band transfer to side arguments. Notation, the fixed version of H, and hypotheses are the same as in the main text. We express its list construction as finite rules that can be enumerated term by term and identify the tests that can be named before catalog insertion begins.

A. The data in a history name

H, p. 47 · H, p. 48 · H, p. 49 · The four history classes in the main text

On a fixed cell, a history name consists of the following finite data: the basic pattern, slot, base name, target mask context and pad, target rank, and ordered list of inner-history factors. A main base name can only be the fixed BeB_eBe​ or UeU_eUe​ in that slot of the selected pattern. A helper base name denotes a complete side state fixed at a later stage.

A factor is specified by four pieces of data: mask context, rank, individual pad, and the choice of a positive projection or its complement. There are two kinds of mask contexts: a cumulative split point mmm, or a historical-ball group GGG together with the insertion index. A history word is an operator product acting from right to left. Equivalently, one may list its successive left multiplications from earliest to latest. The two conventions must not be mixed.

Only currently available columns enter a projection. Columns that may be inserted later are not in its current range. A name specifies how that projection is recomputed each time the catalog grows. Along the sequence of column insertions, the same name determines an increasing family of positive projections. Their complements are not increasing, but can be treated through the positive projection family. This is the monotonicity required by Lemma 6.7.

The target residual is not counted in the inner-history length. For ball targets, the inner histories in all four slots are the original cumulative search words. For transfer targets, the inner history is the remaining positive word exposed by the actual deletion order. A helper target has as its inner history the product of helper averages previously transferred to slot aaa.

  1. The upper retained slot is either the unchanged initial basic BBB, in which case h=Lh=Lh=L, or has as its outermost factor the positive cumulative filter at split point hhh.
  2. When ℓ>1\ell>1ℓ>1, the outermost factor in the lower retained slot is the complementary cumulative filter at split point ℓ−1\ell-1ℓ−1. When ℓ=1\ell=1ℓ=1, there is no lower retained slot.
  3. The two retained slots are distinct. Every new left multiplication acts on a nonretained slot, so an outer factor in a slot still being retained remains unchanged.
  4. Each split point occurs only once along a path. The rank names of all inserted positive factors are distinct, although their numerical values need not be monotone in insertion order.
  5. The total number of cumulative factors in the four slots equals the path length and is at most ⌈log⁡2L⌉\lceil\log_2 L\rceil⌈log2​L⌉.

Any other predetermined rule for choosing a nonretained slot gives the same invariants. We use the least-numbered slot here to make the choice completely specified.

C3. Successive removal of main filters

H, p. 50 · H, p. 51 · Filter deletion order

For each leaf and every role assignment in which c,dc,dc,d both have basic inputs of type BBB, generate all expansions of complementary factors in these two slots. Each such factor contributes either the identity or minus the positive average, so every branch has a definite total sign. Retained positive factors keep their order, and the slot's existing ball average remains outermost.

In each positive-word branch, compare only the two current outermost ranks and remove the larger one. If the outgoing filter is in slot ccc and is denoted by QcQ_cQc​, write VcV_cVc​ for the word after removal. For each individual projection Qc,tQ_{c,t}Qc,t​ of that filter, record

The main list can be generated before helper insertion: the inner words in C3 depend only on the main search and deletion order. A later helper band is an arbitrary observer allowed by the test and need not be included in the main history.

Although all helper factors have the same rank, they generally arise from different masks and need not commute. Catalog termination requires only that the positive projection with a given name increase along the insertion sequence; different names need not give nested projections. A target residual may act on a history of the same rank without violating the requirement that its triggering pure source have lower rank. The actual triggering source always comes from the main slot ccc and has rank at most L+4<R∗L+4<R_*L+4<R∗​.

The vector of the complete side base SaS_aSa​ is fixed before this stage, and its new private helper coordinates are zero. If the base could be reselected during helper insertion, the catalog increments could no longer be assigned to a fixed base, and the hypothesis of Lemma 6.7 would fail. The main and helper lists allow several fixed complete side bases, but not the introduction of infinitely many frequency components as new bases during the construction.

E. What the counting is used for

H, p. 48 · H, p. 49 · Restoring the averages

For a fixed leaf, the four slots have at most m∗m_*m∗​ cumulative factors in total. Hence all complementary expansions in c,dc,dc,d produce at most 2m∗2^{m_*}2m∗​ branches, and there are at most m∗+2m_*+2m∗​+2 deletion positions. Each history factor and target has PPP individual pad choices. Endpoints introduce at most an additional fixed factor of two. There are at most 4!4!4! role assignments and exactly four ball-insertion positions.

The a priori number of original context names is supplied by successful coloring and the historical-mask construction, independently of numerical graph distances. If it is bounded by DCctxD^{C_{\mathrm{ctx}}}DCctx​, equation (10) in the main text is a generous upper bound for the product of all these choices. It does not assert that every listed name is nonzero on some cell, or combine groups from different spatial branches of the full tree into a count along one path.

When the list is used in Lemma 6.7, the number of individual pad histories enters the bound for the total number of insertions. After the catalog has been constructed, the analytic estimate uses averages of total weight one and does not multiply by the number of pad combinations in the list. These two counts have different purposes and must be kept distinct.

Every actual main-list entry consists of projections or complementary projections of main ranks acting on a fixed basic input. Every helper entry consists of positive helper projections acting on a fixed complete side base. The two arbitrary observer arguments require no enumeration; they are already included in the supremum defining the test norm.

F. Quantifiers for the four error types

H, p. 49 · H, p. 50 · H, p. 51 · H, p. 52 · The total far-band error

Fix a finite tree, a pattern, and a set of a priori bounds. The constants below are independent of tree depth, actual individual pads, particular triggering sources, and linear modulation.

  1. Old-source and same-group ball residuals: for each target and individual history, apply equation (13) of H and cancel the Dnr−1D\sqrt{n_{r-1}}Dnr−1​​ coefficient sum of one lower-rank outer support. Only τ3\tau_3τ3​ times the local norms of the fixed base and observers remains.
  2. Mismatches between different groups: expand both the upper-source and first-ball supports. A fixed power of the a priori main-catalog bound is allowed. Distance greater than one between pure sources supplies τ2\tau_2τ2​ by Lemma 6.1. This term does not depend on the old-source residual precision.
  3. First far-band direction: the source is a main band and the target a helper residual. The main-band coefficient bound is at most DnR∗−1D\sqrt{n_{R_*-1}}DnR∗​−1​​ and is canceled by the helper target's denominator. The observers are the other main history and the second side state.
  4. Second far-band direction: the near source comes from the lower-rank main outer factor in the other slot, and the target is the outgoing main residual. The complete helper band is an observer. For far sources, expand the main source and helper support and apply τ2\tau_2τ2​. Only this far-source part uses the helper coefficient bound.

G. Small-parameter boundary cases

H, p. 39 · H, p. 47 · H, p. 49 · H, p. 50 · H, p. 51 · The band identity and both endpoints

When L=1L=1L=1, there is no search split. The initial basic upper source has color one, and there is no lower retained slot. The four ball insertions and subsequent transfers still make sense. No actual split need use cumulative rank one; this does not affect a catalog bound for ranks at most a given value.

When P=1P=1P=1, a positive average is a single projection, but there are still two bands: Q1Q_1Q1​ and 1−Q11-Q_11−Q1​. Equation (15) of H must still be summed through P+1=2P+1=2P+1=2. There are no far bands with ∣p−q∣>2|p-q|>2∣p−q∣>2, and every nonzero difference term is retained as a near-band term.

An empty target mask has zero positive projection. If there is no current group for the first ball, the group sum is empty, and the old-source argument shows that the leaf contributes only a numerical error. If the low helper mask in a transfer is empty, its positive band is zero; no nonexistent neighbor needs to be found.

When two original vertices have the same curvature tag, their graph distance is zero, but their private column labels remain distinct. Cumulative and historical masks determine membership by tag conditions, whereas uniqueness of pure-source coefficients depends on the separate private coordinates. These two meanings of equality must not be confused.

H. Later theorems not supplied by this appendix

H, p. 48 · H, p. 50 · H, p. 51 · H, p. 52 · Scope of the main conclusion

We do not construct the complete side states of Sections 7–8 of H, prove their linear square-sum bounds, or prove a P−1/2P^{-1/2}P−1/2 gain from the near-band sums. We establish only that, given these fixed bases and their a priori number, every test actually used in this chapter belongs to the previously prescribed finite list, and far-band removal does not require the main catalog to change after helper insertion.
