Unit 3, continued: inherited core representatives and first threshold crossings

This part develops the end of D §6.4, pp.51–52, equations (6.16)–(6.17). The later core count needs two kinds of data: frequency separation between distinct representatives along a path, and a bound on the total number of representatives across groups in each size class. These come respectively from inherited representative selection and the square energy of mode projections.

The source uses H Lemmas 8.4, 8.10–8.11, and Proposition 8.12. Under the stated graph-stability and small oscillatory inner-product assumptions, we develop the set-theoretic, almost-orthogonality, and stopping-sum arguments. The rational-height stability theorem from H is not reproved here.

5. Consistent representatives inherited by descendants

Process predecessors of one type in analysis-block order. If the current predecessor contains an earlier successful predecessor on the path, inherit its representative. Otherwise choose the first label in the current predecessor according to a fixed mode-label ordering.

We must check that several earlier predecessors contained in the current one do not require different representatives. Equality of their second type coordinates makes each earlier predecessor contain more than 1/1.11/1.11/1.1 of the current predecessor's modes, hence more than half. Any two earlier predecessors therefore intersect.

They lie on one path, and their common mode connects them. By (Predecessor-inclusion), they are nested in time and, inductively, already share an inherited representative. The rule is therefore consistent.

If representatives on two comparable intervals of actual use differ, their components can contain no coincident modes or modes adjacent in the later CURRENT graph. Otherwise, (Predecessor-inclusion) would force the later component to inherit the earlier representative.

9. The first interval where each representative crosses a threshold

Fix a position, upper height, type, and dyadic threshold

For every group and representative, take on each spatial branch the first interval of actual use satisfying σF≥σ\sigma_F\ge\sigmaσF​≥σ. Call it a first-threshold interval, or top. A group and representative may have several disjoint tops on different branches.

First fix a group GGG and prove an ordinary operator bound. One representative has at most one top along a path.   • Upper good-height selection retains complete core partial sums of minimal CURRENT components;
  • For fixed position, height, and type, representatives are inherited by descendants and satisfy (Representative-gap) for every permitted integer shift;
  • Within a size class, the number of actual representatives across all groups satisfies (Representative-count);

All these statements concern the same actual intervals of use and permanent labels. The quadrilinear sum with at least three core positions has not yet been estimated.
