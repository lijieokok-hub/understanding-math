How the original basic columns are constructed and how many labels a path must reserve

This chapter explains the construction in Proposition 5.6 on pp. 35–36 of H. We use the fixed October 5, 2026 version. All page references below refer to this version.

Chapter 9 requires a bound on the number of raw labels, whereas Chapter 10 bounds the number of active cells. These count different objects. We begin with the greedy construction of the old projections, then insert the prediction columns and the more accurate fitting columns in the high slots. Only then do we combine their counts into the raw budget. The main counting inequality is

Nbase≤Nold+2Nold2Dpred+Na+Nb.(1) N_{\mathrm{base}} \le N_{\mathrm{old}}+2N_{\mathrm{old}}^2D_{\mathrm{pred}}+N_a+N_b. \tag{1} Nbase​≤Nold​+2Nold2​Dpred​+Na​+Nb​.(1)

It uses neither the number of active cells, Proposition 6.8, nor the termination conclusion of Lemma 6.7.

Spatial compression, delayed replacement, and the count of raw labels are proved below and in the appendices. The discrete quadratic inverse theorem used for detection is an external input with its source specified. The proofs of the all-scale Fourier tail bound and the scale summation are in the fixed prediction catalog appendix. Our conclusions concern only the construction of the basic catalog and the inputs it supplies to later arguments.

6. Why a quadruple involves only boundedly many old catalogs

H, p. 34 · H, p. 35

Update the four slots in a fixed cyclic order, and set

kt+1=ktA,A>1. k_{t+1}=k_t^A,\quad A>1. kt+1​=ktA​,A>1.

gt,Ig_{t,I}gt,I​ denotes the fit at update level ttt in the corresponding slot, with value zero before that slot is updated. Fix the earliest two increment levels u<tu<tu<t in distinct slots. Write the corresponding slots as i,ji,ji,j and the remaining slots as a,ba,ba,b. The four full arguments are

Xi=gu−gu−4,Xj=gt−gt−4,Xe=Fe−ge,prev,e∈{a,b}.(7) X_i=g_u-g_{u-4},\quad X_j=g_t-g_{t-4}, \quad X_e=F_e-g_{e,\mathrm{prev}},\quad e\in\{a,b\}. \tag{7} Xi​=gu​−gu−4​,Xj​=gt​−gt−4​,Xe​=Fe​−ge,prev​,e∈{a,b}.(7)

First, at a finite update cutoff, write each actual input as the sum of all earlier increments and the terminal residual. Expand the quadrilinear form. Group every term containing at least two increments by its earliest two levels u<tu<tu<t. In each remaining slot, sum all later increments and the terminal residual back together. This yields exactly the argument in (7)(7)(7), namely Fe−ge,prevF_e-g_{e,\mathrm{prev}}Fe​−ge,prev​. Thus no such term is lost or counted twice. The remaining terms consist either of four terminal residuals or of one full terminal projection and three terminal residuals. By the comparison of earlier and later accuracies in Section 15, the form norm for terminal residuals in two distinct slots tends to zero, while the L2L^2L2 norms of the other two arguments remain uniformly bounded. The remaining terms therefore tend to zero on any fixed finite set of cells. We first fix the finite tree and then let the update cutoff tend to infinity; we do not interchange the infinite-tree limit with the update limit in advance.

For a fixed quadruple, at most six old projections are needed. Each projection has its own labels and inherited pieces. In the common refinement of these boundedly many families of pieces, each new root is either a root of an original piece or the original full root. The sum of root lengths is still bounded by a fixed multiple of the original root length.

Within a common piece, count the slot occurrences in these six catalogs separately. Write the total path budget as NoldN_{\mathrm{old}}Nold​, and use DoldD_{\mathrm{old}}Dold​ to bound their number, their complexity, and their sums of absolute coefficients. Since the parameters of all these levels are at most k=ktk=k_tk=kt​,

Nold≤Dold,log⁡Dold≤kO(1). N_{\mathrm{old}}\le D_{\mathrm{old}},\quad \log D_{\mathrm{old}}\le k^{O(1)}. Nold​≤Dold​,logDold​≤kO(1).

There is no need to take the union of all catalogs from the first level through level ttt.

7. For a fixed pair of low columns, the prediction catalog must be fixed across all scales
