From small-return increment bounds to a rational fast splitting

This chapter reconstructs the geometry-of-numbers argument in the proof of Lemma 4.3 on pages 17–18 of H, restricted to the one-parameter case needed in Lemma 6.1. The source is page 17 of the fixed October 5 version of An L3L^3L3 bound for the trilinear Hilbert transform. Page 41 applies this argument to the conclusion of the two-slot return analysis.

The preceding chapter turns a non-negligible two-slot correlation into a bound on the frequency increment of every sufficiently small return. This is the only analytic input used below. The proof compares successive minima of a body and its polar, bounds the numerators and denominators of a projection, and constructs a nonzero return with a quantitative lower bound on its length. The large real vector VVV and the large real number Λ\LambdaΛ do not enter the final complexity bound.

4. The precise classical input

The only classical result from the geometry of numbers not reproved here is the upper bound in Minkowski's second theorem:

(∏j=1dλj(B,Γ))vol⁡(B)≤2ddet⁡Γ.(Minkowski) \left(\prod_{j=1}^d\lambda_j(B,\Gamma)\right)\operatorname{vol}(B) \le2^d\det\Gamma. \tag{Minkowski} (j=1∏d​λj​(B,Γ))vol(B)≤2ddetΓ.(Minkowski)

Its assumptions are that BBB is a compact convex body centrally symmetric about the origin, with nonempty interior and that Γ\GammaΓ is a full-rank lattice. The source cited by H is Martin Henk, “Successive Minima and Lattice Points”, arXiv:math/0204158v1, Theorem 1.3 on page 2. That source states precisely this one-sided bound. Apply it with B=K,K∘B=K,K^\circB=K,K∘ and Γ=Zd\Gamma=\mathbb Z^dΓ=Zd; both lattice determinants are 111. No stronger polar transference theorem is needed.

We now derive the required transference inequalities from this input.
