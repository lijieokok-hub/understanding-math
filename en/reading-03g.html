Replacement and active patternsD §5.6 · (5.23)–(5.28)

Unit 3, continued: constructing the two delayed projections

This part treats D §5.6, pp.37–40, equations (5.23)–(5.28). The preceding two parts fixed the predictor family and proved the delayed-replacement estimate. We now choose parameters in their order of dependence, construct the actual high-precision projections, and sum over the restarted local constructions.

Local detection from H, the fixed predictor families, and the previously stated operator results remain assumptions. The main checks are that the predictors are fixed before constructing projections in the complementary positions, that a coarse-interval request yields enough projection gain on fine intervals, that old projections remain available for old summands after stopping, and that the two high precisions can be chosen successively without circular dependence.

Suppose a coarse interval III is cut out at depth sss and fine depth h=s+K0h=s+K_0h=s+K0​. Its strict ancestors have smaller delayed projection depths, so the projection values used by their summands have already been fixed.

Existing predictor columns and their private directions persist, so the residual telescoping identities for old ancestor intervals can still be continued to every required fine depth.

Transfer the cut coarse interval and its original candidate descendants to a new construction. Retain the restrictions of the prescribed old data and predictor columns. Predictors already inherited at its root are immediately available; later pairs are still inserted when they first become jointly available, as originally prescribed.

The new construction starts with no additional fitting columns. Old additional fitting columns do not count in its new request history. Its new D0D_0D0​ covers only its prescribed data, and its request bound counts only its own additional requests.

The normalized sequence still satisfies the hypotheses after restriction to the new root. Repeat the coarse-gain, fine-row ordering, and request-bound arguments. The old and new families apply to different original summands. This preserves old projection values while allowing depth-independent estimates to be proved again on each new root.
