Unit 3, continued: three sources of depth variation in local outputs

This part develops D Lemma 6.1 and Corollary 6.2, pp.44–47. A local output contains an outer group projection, an inner ordered product of projections, and an original function that varies with depth. Comparing two depths requires treating these three changes separately.

The frequency-group construction from H enters through a separation inequality on specified endpoint spaces, together with its Compression operator statement. We state these assumptions first and then verify D's varying-function argument. The construction of the groups and endpoint spaces from full curvature labels in H is not reproved here.

2. The endpoint-space assumption from H

Fix one position and one color, and sum only over groups GGG of that color. Group indices remain fixed. Even after a group ceases to occur at a particular depth, its projection family continues by inheriting the original columns and locally reprojecting.

Each individual positive projection family is defined on complete depth partitions. Its range consists of constant-coefficient combinations of main columns, with sets of use inherited as depth increases. For a fixed group and projection choice, the outer projections LG,sL_{G,s}LG,s​ therefore form an orthogonal projection family nested in sss.

An endpoint interval UUU is permitted for group GGG when the corresponding projection's set of use is nonempty and either UUU or a descendant has an actual use of GGG. The source calls this a usable endpoint. Actual use on a finer descendant also establishes usability of its ancestor endpoint.

The endpoint-space separation result from H, used as D equation (6.3), is the following. Suppose each hGh_GhG​ is a finite combination of main columns, each coefficient is constant on a whole permitted endpoint interval, and the column belongs to the corresponding group projection's set of use. Then

∥∑GhG∥22≤Csep∑G∥hG∥22.(Endpoint-separation) \left\|\sum_Gh_G\right\|_2^2 \le C_{\mathrm{sep}}\sum_G\|h_G\|_2^2. \tag{Endpoint-separation} ​G∑​hG​​22​≤Csep​G∑​∥hG​∥22​.(Endpoint-separation)

By choosing the mismatch tolerance after the counting bounds, as in H, one can bound CsepC_{\mathrm{sep}}Csep​ by an absolute constant, independent of kkk and PPP. The same label or interval may recur at different endpoint times. The constant counts neither these repetitions nor the depth of a descendant establishing usability. Column counts, group counts, and private weights must satisfy (Main-sizes) and the separation tolerance chosen in H.

This is an operator statement about combinations of columns on whole intervals. Separation of only the current output functions, or of their restrictions to fine intervals, cannot replace (Endpoint-separation).

3. Ordered projection products in the outputs

An original basic BBB function is a signed sum of at most two current underlying projections. Localization applies further positive projection averages and their complementary operators outside it. Fix a search branch, expand the complementary operators, choose one underlying component, and fix one projection choice in each positive average. This gives

Z^G,s=LG,sBsFs,Bs=Psm⋯Ps1.(One-term) \widehat Z_{G,s}=L_{G,s}\mathcal B_sF_s, \qquad \mathcal B_s=P_s^m\cdots P_s^1. \tag{One-term} ZG,s​=LG,s​Bs​Fs​,Bs​=Psm​⋯Ps1​.(One-term)

Each PsjP_s^jPsj​ is an inherited orthogonal projection family, with main column constant bounded by DmD_{\mathrm m}Dm​. The order is fixed and the length is m=O(log⁡L)m=O(\log L)m=O(logL). These inner families are independent of GGG; projections with distinct jjj need not commute.

A group-independent shared output may have no outer LG,sL_{G,s}LG,s​. The case m=0m=0m=0 is also allowed, with the inner operator product equal to the identity. The source includes the identity output itself among the shared types.

Every required endpoint is evaluated using the complete continued families, even if the group has no current actual use there. When a required finite endpoint lies outside the original function-index range, extend the sequence constantly from the nearest endpoint. Repeating endpoint values does not increase within-block variation. Outputs before the current localization root are still defined to be zero.

For each interval at the left-endpoint depth rrr, denoted by UUU, define a row

8. The third term: changing only the original function

The inner projection product is common to all groups, and every projection is contractive, so

∑G∥1EG,r,tLG,tBt(Ft−Fr)∥22≤Csep∥Bt(Ft−Fr)∥22≤Csep∥1R(Ft−Fr)∥22. \begin{aligned} \sum_G \|\mathbf1_{E_{G,r,t}}L_{G,t}\mathcal B_t(F_t-F_r)\|_2^2 &\le C_{\mathrm{sep}}\|\mathcal B_t(F_t-F_r)\|_2^2\\ &\le C_{\mathrm{sep}}\|\mathbf1_R(F_t-F_r)\|_2^2. \end{aligned} G∑​∥1EG,r,t​​LG,t​Bt​(Ft​−Fr​)∥22​​≤Csep​∥Bt​(Ft​−Fr​)∥22​≤Csep​∥1R​(Ft​−Fr​)∥22​.​

∑[r,t]∈J∥1R(Ft−Fr)∥22≤5a∣R∣. \sum_{[r,t]\in\mathcal J} \|\mathbf1_R(F_t-F_r)\|_2^2\le5a|R|. [r,t]∈J∑​∥1R​(Ft​−Fr​)∥22​≤5a∣R∣.

9. Zero outputs before the root and restoration of the averages

If both endpoints precede the current root, their difference is zero. At most aaa depth intervals cross the starting depth. On these intervals, the earlier output is zero. The right-endpoint projection square sum, inner contraction, and

∥1RFt∥22≤∣R∣ \|\mathbf1_RF_t\|_2^2\le|R| ∥1R​Ft​∥22​≤∣R∣

together bound the cost by Ca∣R∣Ca|R|Ca∣R∣.

Now restore the original positive projection averages. When comparing depths, use the same projection choice in each average so that its projection family stays nested. The choices for shared inner averages must also be common across all groups, keeping the ordered product independent of GGG. Each group's outer projection choices are coupled across the compared depths as well. Regard all choices as one normalized finite probability average and apply Jensen's inequality to the squared norm in the finite row direct sum. This restores the averages without a factor for their number of terms.

Choosing different projections independently at the two depths would make the first term cease to be an increment of one nested family, so the orthogonality argument would no longer apply directly.

Expand complementary operators in the original order into at most 2O(log⁡L)2^{O(\log L)}2O(logL) ordered projection products. The basic BBB has at most two fixed signed components. Restore these finitely many terms using the triangle inequality in the row direct sum, then sum over at most aaa interval colors. This proves (Variation-local) and (Variation-shared).

10. Why all losses remain within G0G_0G0​

By (Main-sizes),

m≤Clog⁡(2k),log⁡log⁡(2Dm)≤Cq+Clog⁡(2k). m\le C\log(2k),\qquad \log\log(2D_{\mathrm m})\le C_q+C\log(2k). m≤Clog(2k),loglog(2Dm​)≤Cq​+Clog(2k).

11. Application to later adjacent projection differences

This is D Corollary 6.2, pp.46–47.

In a one-step output-filter transfer specified by H, removing the outermost positive filter leaves an inner ordered product in the source order, of length O(log⁡L)O(\log L)O(logL) and independent of the group. Its endpoint function is a shared output of the type above. If a permitted group projection remains outside it, the endpoint is of group type.

Let the individual projections in that step be Qp,sQ_{p,s}Qp,s​ for 1≤p≤P1\le p\le P1≤p≤P, and set

Q0,s=0,QP+1,s=1. Q_{0,s}=0,\qquad Q_{P+1,s}=1. Q0,s​=0,QP+1,s​=1.

The source distinguishes two kinds of transfer. In a ball-projection transfer, Qp,sxsQ_{p,s}x_sQp,s​xs​ for 1≤p≤P1\le p\le P1≤p≤P are permitted group endpoints, while xsx_sxs​ is shared. In a cumulative-filter transfer, the outer ball projection has already been removed: xsx_sxs​ and all Qp,sxsQ_{p,s}x_sQp,s​xs​ are shared endpoints.

The complete remaining endpoint xd,sx_{d,s}xd,s​ in the other complementary position also has only two permitted forms. Either it retains an outermost positive ball-projection average with a group-independent inner projection product, or that outer layer has been removed and the output is shared. Apply (Variation-local) in the first case and (Variation-shared) in the second.

For a nonterminal difference

(Qp,s−Qp−1,s)xs, (Q_{p,s}-Q_{p-1,s})x_s, (Qp,s​−Qp−1,s​)xs​,

the two terms are group endpoints or shared endpoints according to the transfer type above. Their difference across depths is controlled by two corresponding proved variation bounds, costing only a fixed factor.

The terminal difference is

xs−QP,sxs. x_s-Q_{P,s}x_s. xs​−QP,s​xs​.

In a ball-projection transfer, count the first term xsx_sxs​ once on the union of all required right-endpoint cells for that shared output; count the second as a permitted group endpoint. In a cumulative-filter transfer, both endpoints are shared, and each is counted on its corresponding union of uses. Repeating the same shared term for every group would not be justified by (Variation-shared).

The total cost for P+1P+1P+1 differences may grow as a fixed polynomial in PPP. Subsequent summation must still preserve the matched combinations specified in the source. The single-endpoint variation bound alone does not permit arbitrary separation of all group and difference terms.
