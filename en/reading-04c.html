Unit 4, continued: Low-dimensional projections of nearly orthogonal tensors

This part studies Theorem 4.1, Lemma 4.2, and their proofs in B §4, pp.11–14. The argument concerns finite-dimensional Hilbert spaces and uses neither mixing, selected lengths, nor distal structure. Its application to ergodic averages with point-dependent lengths comes later, in B §5.

The paper calls the result tensor packing. The quantity being estimated is concrete: among a sequence of nearly orthogonal rank-one tensors, how many can have a large projection onto the same low-dimensional subspace?

1. The full statement

All Hilbert spaces are real, tensor products are Hilbert tensor products, and logarithms are natural. Fix

0<ε≤1,d≥1,L≥0, 0<\varepsilon\le1, \quad d\ge1, \quad L\ge0, 0<ε≤1,d≥1,L≥0,

where d,Ld,Ld,L are integers. Let x1,…,xmx_1,\ldots,x_mx1​,…,xm​ and y1,…,ymy_1,\ldots,y_my1​,…,ym​ lie in the unit balls of H1,H2H_1,H_2H1​,H2​, respectively.

For the xxx sequence, suppose a graph on {1,…,m}\{1,\ldots,m\}{1,…,m} of maximum degree at most LLL contains all pairs satisfying

i≠j,∣⟨xi,xj⟩∣>η i\ne j, \quad |\langle x_i,x_j\rangle|>\eta i=j,∣⟨xi​,xj​⟩∣>η

The yyy sequence satisfies the same condition, possibly with a different exceptional graph. The threshold obeys

0≤η≤ε2/104. 0\le\eta\le\varepsilon^2/10^4. 0≤η≤ε2/104.

B Theorem 4.1 (p.11, equation (4.1)) asserts that there is an absolute constant CCC such that every orthogonal projection of rank at most ddd, denoted P:H1⊗H2→H1⊗H2P:H_1\otimes H_2\to H_1\otimes H_2P:H1​⊗H2​→H1​⊗H2​, satisfies

The constant is independent of both ambient Hilbert-space dimensions. Here ddd bounds the projection rank; it is unrelated to the number of functions in an ergodic average.
