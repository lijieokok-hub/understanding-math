Chapter 2. Saturated array extensions and conditional locality

This chapter follows Definition 3.1, Proposition 3.2, Lemmas 3.3–3.4, and Proposition 3.5 of R (pp. 7–13). It starts with the full family of ordered arrays from Chapter 1. The argument enlarges the local state while preserving conditional product laws, then uses saturation to obtain joint independence of rows and exact conditional kernels for individual cells and grouped rectangles.

Fixed source, from page 7

The complete states at a few positions are added to the parameters. These positions are called parent slots; in a square array, they become parent rows. This additional information is distinct from the original local parameter u(y)u(y)u(y) at each cell. A slot is simply a position indexed by the finite set III.

What remains to be proved

Suppose k≥3k\ge 3k≥3 is the least order at which mixing fails. Mixing below order kkk gives the ordered array laws ρn\rho _{n}ρn​, indexed by InI^{n}In, where I={1,…,k}I=\{1,\ldots,k\}I={1,…,k}. Fixing the last axis index at one slot gives a plane. Under ρn\rho _{n}ρn​, any family of at most k−1k-1k−1 last-axis planes is independent as a family of complete random vectors. Independence here means a joint product law, beyond pairwise independence.

The ordered limits first give this conclusion along the last axis. For n=2n=2n=2, this is independence of proper subfamilies of whole columns; the corresponding statement for whole rows is still unavailable. Interchanging rows and columns cannot supply it: the source explicitly assumes no symmetry under permutations of the axes.

Section 3 of R enlarges both the local states and their parameters while keeping the resulting object in the specified array class. Saturation then reduces the new observations in the extension to observations in the original object. This is how row independence is obtained. The difficulty is that independence can be lost after conditioning on additional information. The proof below spells out the local dependence of conditional kernels that prevents this problem.

Closure under inverse limits

Consider a countable tower of extensions. Take the compatible path spaces for the local states and the parameters, with time acting coordinatewise. Countable products of standard Borel spaces, and their Borel subsets of compatible paths, are again standard Borel. The consistent finite-stage laws determine the limit probability laws. Commutativity of the factor diagrams, face consistency along every axis, and time invariance hold first for finite-stage tests and then at the limit by monotone-class approximation.

Unconditional independence passes to the limit in the same way: pull all tests on finitely many planes back from one common finite stage, factor the product expectation there, and then approximate arbitrary bounded tests on the limit. The argument also preserves independence of the local XXX coordinate κ(y)\kappa (y)κ(y) from the parameters.

7. Conull sets and quantifiers

All state spaces are standard Borel, so regular conditional distributions exist, and a countable generating algebra can determine each probability kernel. Verify a kernel identity first on this determining class, then obtain equality of probability measures on one conull set. This supplies the common set needed to use the identity for bounded tests, beyond separate almost-everywhere statements for individual tests.

There are countably many levels nnn. For fixed kkk, there are only finitely many parent subsets, projection positions, missing positions, and square groupings; integer time shifts are countable. The required kernel identities can therefore hold simultaneously on a common conull set at each relevant level. When time covariance is needed, intersect over all integer shifts as well.

Random variables at different levels live in different spaces, so common conull sets are understood level by level. At n=2n=2n=2, disintegrating over the law of the full parameter EEE gives a conull parameter set. For every EEE in that set, the assertions hold almost everywhere under its conditional square law. They need not hold at every state point over that parameter.

The proof does not give any of the following stronger assertions:

  • “Saturation holds for all extensions on one common pointwise conull set.”
  • “Conditional independence automatically survives the addition of parent slots.”
  • “Column independence gives row independence by interchanging the axes.”
  • “The extended TYT_YTY​ is still mixing.”
  • “Product laws for every proper subfamily imply a product law for the complete kkk-tuple.”

The source retains mixing only in the XXX coordinate. The enlarged system YYY is not required to be mixing.

Aside: why column independence alone is insufficient

The following example shows why a shortcut omitting saturation can fail. It is not a counterexample to R, and no claim is made that this finite square extends to the full array tower required there.

Take k=3k=3k=3. Let UaU_aUa​ and VaV_aVa​, for a=1,2,3a=1,2,3a=1,2,3, together with HHH, be seven independent fair bits. With ⊕\oplus⊕ denoting addition modulo two, define

ya,1=Ua,ya,2=Va,ya,3=Ua⊕Va⊕H. y_{a,1}=U_a,\quad y_{a,2}=V_a,\quad y_{a,3}=U_a\oplus V_a\oplus H. ya,1​=Ua​,ya,2​=Va​,ya,3​=Ua​⊕Va​⊕H.

The three entries in each row are independent: an output triple in a fixed row uniquely determines UaU_aUa​, VaV_aVa​, and HHH, so it has probability 1/8. The three entries in each column are also independent. Any two whole columns are independent. This is immediate for the first two. Given the first column, the third is the independent uniform vector VVV translated by a vector, and its uniform law is independent of the first column. The second and third columns are treated in the same way. The parameters may be constant.

Yet the parity ya,1⊕ya,2⊕ya,3y_{a,1}\oplus y_{a,2}\oplus y_{a,3}ya,1​⊕ya,2​⊕ya,3​ of every row is the same variable HHH. Observing one complete parent row PPP therefore determines HHH. After the first two whole columns are observed as well, the missing entry of another row AAA is

Its conditional law is a point mass. Under the marginal law of row AAA alone, given yA,1,yA,2y_{A,1},y_{A,2}yA,1​,yA,2​, the third entry is still a fair bit. Thus the two sides of (12) can differ in this finite example. The original column product laws do not justify (12). R first derives locality of the conditional kernels from saturation and extensions with smaller parent sets, and then obtains (12). That part of the local argument is necessary.
