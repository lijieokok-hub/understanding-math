Why three fixed basic columns leave only finitely many active scales

This chapter gives a close reading of Lemma 5.7 and Proposition 5.8 on pp. 37–38 of H. The fixed source is An L3L^3L3 bound for the trilinear Hilbert transform, October 5, 2026 version.

The catalog-termination argument in Chapter 9 needs two different counts: the number of original columns on a root-to-leaf path, and the number of active cells on that path. Having few original columns does not mean that the path is short. This chapter explains how oscillation of three fixed columns and the marginal cancellation of the kernel yield the second count, and states exactly where the first count comes from.

We start from the basic data already constructed on p. 37. Their construction and replacement errors in Proposition 5.6 on pp. 35–36 remain source inputs; this chapter does not infer that construction from the conclusions below. We develop the scale estimate for three fixed columns here. The three-variable analytic calculation is in the appendix to this chapter.

A companion proof of the source input above is now developed in H11: basic-column construction. Section 16 explains its original path-budget output. The proof retains the external discrete inverse theorem and explicit kernel conditions and does not use this chapter’s active-cell conclusion. The parameter note supplies a quantitative completion for the conventions on pp. 31 and 35. Actual later histories, whole helper bases, and global absorption are still not included.

5. Why bounded rational height controls the number of scales

H source, p. 13 · H source, p. 14 · This chapter: appendix, Section 6

We first state the conclusion of Lemma 3.5 on pp. 13–14 that is actually used. Fix ξ∈Rd\xi\in\mathbb R^dξ∈Rd, and consider all nonzero values produced by vectors whose entries have rational height at most B≥2B\ge2B≥2:

Minimal dependence ensures that the null vector obtained from these minors has no zero component.

Set Ld=4dHdL_d=4dH_dLd​=4dHd​. If the absolute values of a sequence of nonzero evaluations are successively separated by factors greater than LdL_dLd​, their rational vectors must be linearly independent. Otherwise, take a minimally dependent subfamily. In its integer relation, the term with the largest evaluation has absolute value at least that evaluation's absolute value, whereas the sum of the absolute values of all other terms is less than

dHd/Ld=1/4 dH_d/L_d=1/4 dHd​/Ld​=1/4

allows only one depth interval for each such interval, of length

ℓ−1(log⁡2Ld+log⁡2(β/α)) \ell^{-1}\bigl(\log_2L_d+\log_2(\beta/\alpha)\bigr) ℓ−1(log2​Ld​+log2​(β/α))

9. Active cells and the root-to-leaf path count

Now fix a sufficiently large absolute constant AAA and set

13. What is preserved on restriction to a subroot

The active cells need not form an ancestor-closed tree. When a tree is needed, add the root and the cells connecting the active cells to it. The budget MMM counts only active cells; there may still be many connecting cells. The original budget n0n_0n0​ must cover all original births on each complete path, including columns born on connecting cells and first used at deeper active cells.

When restricting to a subroot R′R'R′ that already satisfies the required local normalization, keep the original functions, private labels, and lists. Labels already available above the subroot are inherited at the new root and are not charged again. Births below the subroot must not be used in the new root's projection in advance. Each new path extends to an original path, so the upper bounds for n0n_0n0​ and MMM remain valid. The orthogonal square-sum estimate in Section 11 can be proved again on R′R'R′.

When later removing filters from the two nonside slots, one must return to this tuple's original whole BBB arguments. For these two slots p,qp,qp,q, the identity

BpBq=YpYq−UpYq−YpUq+UpUq B_pB_q=Y_pY_q-U_pY_q-Y_pU_q+U_pU_q Bp​Bq​=Yp​Yq​−Up​Yq​−Yp​Uq​+Up​Uq​

14. Parameter order and the scope left open by this chapter

H source, p. 35 · H source, p. 36 · H source, p. 37 · H source, p. 38 · H source, p. 39 · H source, p. 49 · This chapter: appendix, Section 7

First fix the basic catalog, K0K_0K0​, local sizes, and full-argument pair-smallness supplied by Proposition 5.6. Then choose DDD to control these basic data. The exponent in the fixed-triple estimate depends only on the atom-complexity rules and fixed kernel class, not on real frequencies, tree depth, the number of branches, or the birth locations of the triple.

Next fix the active-threshold exponent AAA. Only then, for each required threshold δ\deltaδ, choose the Weyl-splitting budget, Fourier truncation, and small/large frequency gap. The actual splitting and truncation may vary by cell, but their rational heights must lie in a uniformly fixed range. Finally increase the starting kkk to absorb fixed polynomial factors and set MMM and the original budget n0n_0n0​ used in Chapter 9. New template insertions in Chapter 9 do not redefine activity in this chapter or add to its original vertices.

Under these explicit basic-data assumptions, this chapter proves the fixed-triple large-value scale count, small-value path sum, active path budget, and minimum reduction after removing inactive cells and the two-difference term. The appendix derives the three-variable Weyl estimate and finite chart expansion.

The parts not reconstructed here are the actual construction in Proposition 5.6 and of earlier original catalogs, the spatial compression in the full Lemma 3.4, the actual localization errors and history lists in Proposition 6.8, the whole helper bases in Section 7, and the global absorption argument for H. In particular, the numerical budget for n0n_0n0​ still comes from the basic construction on p. 37. This chapter only aligns that source with the counting objects actually needed later.
