Comparing a short depth block with the same finite-depth constant

This chapter gives a close reading of Lemma 7.1 and Lemma 7.2 on pp. 52–57 of H, using the October 5, 2026 version.

The preceding chapter split the transfer errors for the two complementary arguments into far and near bands. Far bands yield a locally small error. Near bands still have to be estimated within a short depth block, where the inputs usually have only normalized square-integral bounds on terminal leaves. Why three direct approaches fall short

The following reconstruction explains the logic of the argument. It is not an account of how the authors actually discovered it.

A first natural approach is to upgrade each leafwise L2L^2L2 bound directly to an LqL^qLq bound and then use CN(q)C_N(q)CN​(q). A second approach is to use an absolute estimate at each depth. This gives CK∣R∣CK|R|CK∣R∣, but its exponent does not become small as q↓2q\downarrow2q↓2. Even if all four slots are clipped first, crude interpolation from L2L^2L2 to LpjL^{p_j}Lpj​ leaves a product of clipping factors equal to A∑j(1−2/pj)=A2A^{\sum_j(1-2/p_j)}=A^2A∑j​(1−2/pj​)=A2, because ∑j1/pj=1\sum_j1/p_j=1∑j​1/pj​=1. We therefore need to confine the potentially heavy input to at most one slot and obtain higher integrability for the other slots by a different argument.

A third approach is to randomize the frequencies independently in the four slots. This may improve each individual moment, but it generally destroys the original four-factor integral. The two linear constraints in (29) suggest looking for a shared phase whose total phase vanishes on their nullspace, while its single-slot fourth-moment expansion eliminates unpaired resonances. The quadratic phase in (28) does both.

The leaf-depth rectangles ensure that there is at most one heavy slot. Quadratic randomization supplies fourth-order integrability in the ordinary slots, leaving only the exception to be clipped. The two sampling steps place these operations on a fast torus and then return to the original kernel and the same CN(q)C_N(q)CN​(q). We now work through this argument.
