Unit 4, Part II: Conditional laws given all distal parameters

Part I constructed the selected law λY\lambda_YλY​ and proved its one-coordinate marginals and invariance. Here we study parts (2) and (3) of B Proposition 3.2 and Lemma 3.3, pp.9–10. Why is the full parameter law unaffected by length selection? Why do the states in every proper subset have a product conditional law even after all parameters are given?

The structural results in B Proposition 2.2 and the pointwise theorem for at most three factors in Lemma 2.1 are explicit hypotheses. We derive the conditional laws under these assumptions. We do not reprove Furstenberg–Zimmer theory, the nonergodic distal pointwise theorem, or all the cited literature.

1. Structural definitions and inputs

1.1 Relative weak mixing

Source: B §2.2, p.4, equation (2.2).

Let (Y,m,R)(Y,m,R)(Y,m,R) be a standard invertible probability-preserving system, and let B\mathcal BB be a factor invariant under both RRR and R−1R^{-1}R−1, viewed as a σ\sigmaσ-algebra. This controls averaged conditional correlations after the part determined by the factor has been removed. The time average remains essential; the statement does not assert decay at each individual time.

1.2 Relative compactness and measure-distality

Source: B p.4, the definitions following (2.2).

For finitely many F1,…,Fr∈L2(m)F_1,\ldots,F_r\in L^2(m)F1​,…,Fr​∈L2(m), consider the finitely generated L∞(B)L^\infty(\mathcal B)L∞(B)-module

It is invariant if composition with both RRR and R−1R^{-1}R−1 preserves this space. The extension is relatively compact if the linear span of all such invariant finitely generated modules is dense in L2(m)L^2(m)L2(m). Individual modules need not be closed in L2L^2L2; closure is taken at the end.

A relatively distal extension is built successively from relatively compact extensions and inverse limits. Without a specified base, distal means distal over the trivial factor. This is a measure-theoretic definition: no metric is chosen on YYY, and a condition on distances between pairs of points does not replace it. Nonergodic systems are allowed. For example, the identity transformation preserves every one-dimensional subspace and hence is a compact system.

1.3 The precise scope of B Proposition 2.2

Source: B pp.4–6, the five parts of Proposition 2.2 and the subsequent flow version.

We invoke the following structural results, all without an additional ergodicity assumption.

  1. Every standard system has a maximal measure-distal factor WYW_YWY​ containing its invariant factor, and Y→WYY\to W_YY→WY​ is relatively weakly mixing. The maximal distal factor of an extension contains the pullback of the old distal factor. Every measure-preserving automorphism commuting with the transformation preserves WYW_YWY​.
  2. Factors and joinings of distal systems are distal. A distal system is relatively distal over each of its factors. For every nonzero integer qqq, the transformation RRR is distal if and only if RqR^qRq is distal.
  3. Relative weak mixing passes to nonzero integer powers. Over any invariant joining of the bases, taking the conditional product of the extension fibers produces a relatively weakly mixing extension.
  4. A relatively weakly mixing extension and a relatively distal extension over the same base are relatively disjoint: every invariant joining identifying their base coordinates must make the two systems independent conditional on that base.
  5. For a distal transformation RRR, every fixed d≥1d\ge1d≥1, and bounded functions g1,…,gdg_1,\ldots,g_dg1​,…,gd​,
1N∑n=1N∏j=1dgj(Rjny) \frac1N\sum_{n=1}^{N}\prod_{j=1}^{d}g_j(R^{jn}y) N1​n=1∑N​j=1∏d​gj​(Rjny)

converges almost everywhere. No general product-of-integrals limit is asserted.

For a probability-preserving flow SYtS_Y^tSYt​, the maximal distal factor WYW_YWY​ of the time-one map is preserved by the whole flow, and its action at every nonzero rational time is distal. Statements about the factor action are understood at each fixed time modulo null sets. Time integrals are always computed on the original jointly Borel flow YYY.

B attributes existence and the relative structure to Furstenberg–Zimmer theory and its cited sources, and discusses the stability properties on pp.5–6. The ergodic case of part (5) comes from Huang–Shao–Ye Theorem C; B p.6 separately discusses the nonergodic decomposition. Citing the ergodic HSY theorem alone does not supply the full nonergodic version needed here.

6.2 The full parameter system is relatively distal over the same base

7. The normalized parameters still lie in the maximal distal factor

Source: B Lemma 3.3, p.10, immediately after (3.7).

Return to the normalized flow from Part I:

Mb(Y)=(Y4,λY,(yi)i↦(SYit/byi)i),b∈I. M_b(Y)=\left(Y^4,\lambda_Y, (y_i)_i\mapsto(S_Y^{it/b}y_i)_i\right), \quad b\in I. Mb​(Y)=(Y4,λY​,(yi​)i​↦(SYit/b​yi​)i​),b∈I.

On parameter iii, the time-one action is SWYi/bS_{W_Y}^{i/b}SWY​i/b​. This rational-time action is distal. Indeed, for r=p/q≠0r=p/q\ne0r=p/q=0,

(SWYp/q)q=SWYp. (S_{W_Y}^{p/q})^q=S_{W_Y}^p. (SWY​p/q​)q=SWY​p​.

The right side is an integer power of the distal time-one action, so it is distal. A transformation is distal if and only if a nonzero integer power is distal. Therefore the underlying transformation SWYp/qS_{W_Y}^{p/q}SWY​p/q​ on the left is also distal.

The full parameter array is an invariant joining of these rational-speed distal systems, hence is itself a distal factor. By maximality, it lies in the maximal distal factor of the time-one map of Mb(Y)M_b(Y)Mb​(Y).

Under further iteration, the pullback of an old distal factor to an extension remains contained in the new maximal distal factor. Repeated application proves the conclusion of Lemma 3.3 for the original parameter arrays at every finite stage.

Phase parameters also lie in these factors. The time-one map of the unit-roof suspension preserves the phase uuu, so the phase factor has the identity action and is compact. The phase weight that retained the positive correlation in Part I can therefore be used as a function of the full distal parameter.

8. Order of the deduction and remaining dependencies

The order of the argument matters.

  1. Use pointwise convergence for at most three factors to identify the proper-subset selected-length law with the deterministic-length law.
  2. Apply relative L2L^2L2 replacement to deterministic-length averages, obtaining the conditional product given the subset's own parameters.
  3. Show that the state system is relatively weakly mixing over the parameter subarray, while the full parameter system is relatively distal over that same subarray.
  4. Use relative disjointness to extend the conditioning from the subarray to the full parameter array.

The conditional product in the second step alone does not imply the fourth. Nor may relative L2L^2L2 replacement be applied directly at lengths depending on the starting point.

The structural material still requiring independent verification is the full scope of B Proposition 2.2, especially the nonergodic distal pointwise theorem and relative disjointness, together with the analytic proof used for B Lemma 2.1. This part does not use the assertion that ordinary mixing implies mixing of every finite order. That companion result enters separately when the auxiliary-group structure is maintained later.
