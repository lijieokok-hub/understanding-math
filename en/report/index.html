Norm convergence and pointwise convergence

Existing multiple norm-convergence theory concerns limits in L². For these distinct nonzero integer slopes, weak mixing suffices to identify the norm limit as the product of integrals. The new claims address pointwise convergence. Mixing does not make these observations independent samples, so an independent-variable law of large numbers is not a direct argument.

A limit need not be a product

Modulo 3, the time 3n is always zero, so the third factor is 1_{0}(x). If x is nonzero, every product vanishes. If x=0, the first two factors both equal one exactly when n is divisible by 3, with frequency one third. Each individual integral is one third. Correlations of {0} differ along n=3k and n=3k+1, so the system is not mixing.

Triple averages: first obtain a limit

Persistent nonconvergence on a positive-measure set produces arbitrarily many time blocks, each contributing a fixed positive amount of oscillation. This gives a lower bound linear in the number of blocks, contradicting the sublinear upper bound in the manuscripts. Smoothing and recovery of discrete lengths remain necessary. Mixing is used to identify the final limit; existence and identification are distinct steps.

H: the trilinear Hilbert transform

The principal value uses symmetric truncation at the singularity. The scope here is fixed slopes 1, 2, 3 and the L³×L³×L³ to L¹ bound, not all exponents or all slopes. A unique bounded extension as a function-space operator is distinct from pointwise existence of principal values for every input.

The local quadrilinear estimate in H uses some q in (2,3) and controls the number of depths when the input is fixed across depths. D additionally controls depth-varying inputs and their variation budget, choosing a common q in the parameter order of the proof in H. The existence of some q in H Theorem 2.2 does not permit an arbitrary choice of q. It would be inaccurate to replace this by “the main theorem of H directly implies triple pointwise convergence.” We explain the role of the input without reproducing all technical lemmas.

Higher order: conditional laws after selection

A uses pointwise convergence for at most three distinct positive integer slopes on standard invertible probability-preserving auxiliary systems, obtained from C’s oscillation and profile-approximation arguments. This input requires no mixing and asserts no product limit.
