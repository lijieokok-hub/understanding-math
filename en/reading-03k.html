Localization and control across depthsD §6.4 · (6.12)

Unit 3, continued: how bad-height counts control square sums of mode rows

This part treats the first half of D §6.4, pp.49–50, especially equation (6.12). The preceding frozen-row theorem assumes an ordinary joint Bessel bound for each group's mode projections. We show why the non-core mode rows actually selected in the source satisfy this hypothesis, then pass from orthogonal projections to exact partial sums of mode coefficients.

D uses the graphs, good/bad classification, and geometric conclusions of H §§8.4–8.6. Two source assumptions remain explicit here: the bounds on exceptional blocks and bad heights for each main column, and the small oscillatory inner products between nonadjacent modes. Their rational-height stability proofs and Fourier estimates are not all reproved here. The chain count, the deduction of Bessel bounds from small inner products, and the subsequent argument for exact partial sums are developed below.

2. Good heights, core modes, and non-core modes

The source distinguishes whether a main column is exceptional in the current block and, for a nonexceptional column, whether height stability holds at hhh. These tests come from H Lemmas 8.2–8.3 (pp.67–69). The quantitative conclusions used here are:

  • A fixed main column is exceptional in at most Bbad≤Cdmax⁡B_{\mathrm{bad}}\le C d_{\max}Bbad​≤Cdmax​ blocks along a path;
  • In a fixed block, a nonexceptional main column fails stability at at most Abad≤Cdmax⁡RhtA_{\mathrm{bad}}\le C d_{\max}R_{\mathrm{ht}}Abad​≤Cdmax​Rht​ heights.

Here dmax⁡d_{\max}dmax​ controls the relevant horizontal parameter dimension, and RhtR_{\mathrm{ht}}Rht​ is an auxiliary integer in the rational-height argument. It may depend on the larger mode constant. That dependence is absorbed first by choosing the total height J∗J_*J∗​.

For h≥Δh\ge\Deltah≥Δ, a mode mmm is good at height hhh if

⌊log⁡1.1∣π(Eh−Δ(m))∣⌋=⌊log⁡1.1∣π(Fh(m))∣⌋, \left\lfloor\log_{1.1}|\pi(\mathsf E_{h-\Delta}(m))|\right\rfloor = \left\lfloor\log_{1.1}|\pi(\mathsf F_h(m))|\right\rfloor, ⌊log1.1​∣π(Eh−Δ​(m))∣⌋=⌊log1.1​∣π(Fh​(m))∣⌋,

and at least nine tenths of the main columns in π(Fh(m))\pi(\mathsf F_h(m))π(Fh​(m)) are nonexceptional in the block and stable at that height. All heights below Δ\DeltaΔ are bad.

Fix a sufficiently small absolute constant δ∗>0\delta_*>0δ∗​>0. A mode is core if it has at most δ∗(J∗+1)\delta_*(J_*+1)δ∗​(J∗​+1) bad heights. Otherwise it is non-core, called a linear-side mode in the source.

Modes in the same minimal PRE component have the same component in every higher PRE and CURRENT graph. Their good/bad classifications are therefore identical: an entire minimal PRE component is either core or non-core. This elementary inheritance fact suffices for selecting the non-core events below.

3. Chains of non-core events

Fix a position and a group. An event is a triple

(b,J,E), (b,J,E), (b,J,E),

where JJJ is a retained depth-vbv_bvb​ interval and EEE is a non-core minimal PRE component in its frozen list.

Split analysis-block indices into ten residue classes modulo ten. Within one class, connect an earlier event to a later event when the latter lies on a descendant path and some modes in the two events coincide or are adjacent in the later event's minimal PRE graph.

Successive blocks in one class are separated by at least 10K10K10K, so the later PRE comparison depth is no earlier than the previous block's end. Every CURRENT edge from the earlier block persists in the later PRE graph: the interval is smaller, the threshold is wider, and the modes and integer-shift witnesses are retained. Minimal PRE connections persist at every height.

6. An ordinary Bessel bound at each chain level

Assign each event the length of the longest chain ending there. Connected events have different levels, so overlapping events at the same level have neither coincident nor adjacent modes. Distinct events on the same interval in the same block are already distinct minimal PRE components, with no edges between them.

Along any path, mode-label sets of same-level events are therefore disjoint and have total size at most D∗D_*D∗​. The oscillatory part of H Lemma 8.7 further states that any mode pair from two overlapping events has normalized inner product on the smaller interval of absolute value at most

D∗−200.(Mode-pair) D_*^{-200}. \tag{Mode-pair} D∗−200​.(Mode-pair)

This analytic smallness follows in the source from PRE nonadjacency, permitted integer shifts, and cutoff Fourier tails. It is the second explicit geometric assumption used here.

9. Square energy of the non-core part

On one interval JJJ, distinct minimal PRE components have disjoint labels and satisfy the same small cross-mode inner-product bounds. The coefficient calculation in Section 6.1 therefore also applies to their exact partial sums, bounding the squared norm of their sum by the sum of their squared norms.

Define Vl,G,bV_{l,G,b}Vl,G,b​ as the exact sum of all non-core modes in Z~l,G,b\widetilde Z_{l,G,b}Zl,G,b​, including private coordinates, and set it to zero on branches with no required output or basis for use. Sum over disjoint intervals JJJ at one depth and over all G,bG,bG,b. This gives D equation (6.12):

∑G,b∥Vl,G,b∥22≤G0∣R∣.(Side-energy) \sum_{G,b}\|V_{l,G,b}\|_2^2\le G_0|R|. \tag{Side-energy} G,b∑​∥Vl,G,b​∥22​≤G0​∣R∣.(Side-energy)

The entire argument holds for a fixed but arbitrary candidate offset. Later, the physical approximation error may first be averaged and then a suitable offset selected; the uniform square-energy bound survives. Nor does the proof require one mode to have identical frozen coefficients in different blocks.
