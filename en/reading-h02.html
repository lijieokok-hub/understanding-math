Historical ball masks and Bessel bounds for whole endpoint spaces

This chapter gives a close reading of Lemmas 6.4–6.5 in H, on pages 43–46 of the fixed October 5 version of An L3L^3L3 bound for the trilinear Hilbert transform. Starting with successfully chosen colour data, we prove the geometric properties of the masks and explain why the same endpoint and column label may be used repeatedly.

There are two explicit inputs: the successful marking data on pages 42–43 and the small-inner-product estimate on whole cells from Lemma 6.1. Existence of the colouring and curvature detection remain inputs to this chapter.

1. Graph distances, birth labels, and fixed random arrays

5. A mask retains historical centres

For an integer 1≤h≤Hmax⁡1\le h\le H_{\max}1≤h≤Hmax​, a currently available vertex bbb belongs to MG,hM_{G,h}MG,h​ if and only if there is a historical centre v0v_0v0​ on the same path, assigned the same colour and group at the current cell or an ancestor, such that

The mask is empty before the first centre assignment. Within one stage, the score condition improves with depth, the pseudometrics are fixed, and historical witnesses remain available. The masks therefore increase along the path.

After the stage ends, its qualifying sets may be inherited if nested operators are needed. But that stage has no further actual use on the path. A centre born on another branch cannot serve as a historical witness for this branch.

An endpoint is usable if its mask is nonempty and the group has an actual centre use at that cell or a descendant. This witness of actual use need not be at the endpoint itself. A usable endpoint is still within its own stage; a set retained only by inheritance after the stage ends has no future witness of actual use.

16. What has been proved

For successfully fixed marking data, the historical masks contain the centre balls, are closed under adjacency with an additional pad level, admit short paths at entry, and separate overlapping usable endpoints. Adding the whole-cell small-inner-product input and the bounds on path counts and private weights yields the Bessel bound for whole endpoint spaces.

The exceptional-set bound for successful marking, curvature detection in Lemma 6.1, finite closure of the column catalog, and the later two-sided estimates require separate arguments. The calculations in this chapter do not remove those conditions.
