Appendix: why a local residual detects a column

4. The discrete inverse theorem is a specified external input

We use the s=2s=2s=2 case of Leng–Sah–Sawhney's Theorem 1.2, arXiv:2402.17994v3, p. 1: for 0<δ<1/20<\delta<1/20<δ<1/2, if a 1-bounded function on an interval has U3U^3U3 norm at least δ\deltaδ, then its correlation with a polynomial nilsequence adapted to a filtration of degree at most two has a quasipolynomial lower bound. The dimension is polynomial in the logarithm of the inverse threshold, while the complexity, Lipschitz norm, and inverse correlation bound are quasipolynomial. Definitions 3.3–3.5 on pp. 11–12 specify a rational Malcev basis, the lattice of integer coordinates of the second kind, and a Lipschitz norm that includes a supremum-norm term. The polynomial orbit must be adapted to the stated filtration; see Definitions 2.5–2.7, pp. 7–8 and Definition 3.14, p. 15. We use an unconjugated correlation pairing. Conjugating the output function in the original theorem makes this change without altering the correlation lower bound, Lipschitz norm, or complexity.

This is the only deep inverse theorem not reproved in this chapter. The passage from that theorem to continuous chart atoms below follows pp. 23–26 of H. Large real orbit parameters must not enter the complexity.
