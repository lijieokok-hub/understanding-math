Unit 3, continued: summing scales after separation and fixing the predictor family

This part treats D §§5.3–5.4, pp.30–34, Lemma 5.3 and Corollary 5.4. The preceding quadrilinear expansion leaves two projection differences and two residual positions. We first fix two scalar structured functions from the projection differences and construct a family of functions for subsequently approximating the residuals. This family must be chosen before the functions in the other two positions and their projection constructions.

The argument uses scalar separation, uniform truncation, frequency counting, and fixed-function Bessel estimates from H. We identify these statements individually and explain how D applies them to depth-varying functions. The scalar results from H remain explicit assumptions here.

6. Why the mean-zero tensor expansion admits smooth-row estimates

For the linear-phase part, first absorb e(γt)\mathrm e(\gamma t)e(γt) into two fixed multipliers, then express the unmodulated kernel wIw_IwI​ in coordinates (ya,yb)(y_a,y_b)(ya​,yb​). The absolute Jacobian is ∣b−a∣|b-a|∣b−a∣, fixed and nonzero. The four-direction cancellation condition includes directions a,ba,ba,b, so integrating the transformed kernel in either variable with the other fixed gives zero.

Integrate once in each variable to obtain a double primitive. The zero marginal integrals ensure that it vanishes outside a rectangle strictly inside I×II\times II×I. Expand this primitive in a smooth Fourier series on a slightly larger fixed rectangle, multiply by an interior cutoff equal to one on its support, and differentiate in both variables. This gives a tensor expansion of the original kernel.

Each one-variable function is the derivative of a compactly supported function and therefore has integral exactly zero. Fixed-order derivative bounds grow at most polynomially in the full Fourier index, while the coefficients decay faster than the reciprocal of any fixed polynomial. We may therefore prove the row estimate at one fixed full tensor index, then sum absolutely over the indices.
