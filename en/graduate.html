Why do several observation times make one orbit harder to understand?

Single-function ergodic averages provide a starting point for multiple averages. These notes develop the question, the key pointwise-convergence argument, and the connections between the four papers.

Sections 1–9 concern triple averages; §§10–13 turn to fourfold and arbitrary-finite-order averages. Measure-theoretic and dynamical notions are introduced where needed.

1. Begin with one observable

4. What does mixing imply, and what remains to prove?

A transformation TTT is mixing if, for every two fixed measurable sets E,FE,FE,F,

μ(E∩T−rF)→μ(E)μ(F)\mu (E\cap T^{-r}F) \to \mu (E)\mu (F)μ(E∩T−rF)→μ(E)μ(F)as ∣r∣→∞|r|\to \infty∣r∣→∞.

For bounded functions, the equivalent formulation is ∫g⋅(h∘Tr) dμ⟶(∫g dμ)(∫h dμ)\int g\cdot(h\circ T^r)\,\mathrm{d}\mu\longrightarrow\left(\int g\,\mathrm{d}\mu\right)\left(\int h\,\mathrm{d}\mu\right)∫g⋅(h∘Tr)dμ⟶(∫gdμ)(∫hdμ). It follows first for simple functions from the set formulation, then for general bounded functions by uniform approximation.

Approaching independence here refers to a limiting two-observation correlation. It asserts neither exact independence at finite times nor joint independence of three, four, or more readings without further argument.

5. What does each of the four papers claim?

These are claims in four OpenAI preprints dated October 4, 2026; their full proof chains have not been independently verified. The common assumptions are an invertible, bimeasurable, mixing, probability-preserving transformation and fixed bounded observables. The original probability space need not be standard, and no mixing rate is assumed. The claimed almost-everywhere convergence runs through all positive integer lengths NNN, with the product of the integrals as limit.

D · The basic triple average

Slopes 1, 2, 3. The analytic task is to extend local estimates for fixed inputs to inputs varying with scale depth, in order to control oscillation.

Pointwise convergence of triple ergodic averages for mixing transformations, Theorem 1.1, p.2.

C · Triple averages with arbitrary distinct integer speeds

Any fixed triple of pairwise distinct nonzero integer slopes, such as −2,1,5-2,1,5−2,1,5, with signs allowed to differ. Constants may depend on the slopes. The proof needs modifications to the integer configuration, beyond replacing TTT by a power.

Triple ergodic averages with distinct integer slopes, Theorem 1.1, p.1.

B · Fourfold averages

Slopes 1, 2, 3, 4. The argument selects lengths, constructs joint laws, preserves precise independence constraints, and uses extensions and regrouping to obtain a contradiction.

Pointwise convergence of fourfold ergodic averages for mixing transformations, Theorem 1.1, p.1.

A · Any fixed finite order

For every fixed d≥2d\ge 2d≥2, the slopes are 1,…,d1,\ldots ,d1,…,d. It extends the fourfold construction while retaining three auxiliary groups; the number of groups does not grow with the number of factors.

Pointwise Multiple Ergodic Averages for Mixing Transformations, Theorem 1.1, p.1.

Why specify that the space need not be standard?

A standard probability space can, up to null-set issues, be modeled on a standard Borel space, for example a Borel probability measure on a Polish space. Many structure theorems and conditional-distribution methods are stated there. The four papers do not require the original space to be standard. For finitely many fixed functions, encode all integer translates in a countable product of compact disks to obtain a standard Borel factor. Prove the conclusion on that factor and pull it back. Only countably many observations of the fixed functions are involved; this does not supply a common exceptional set for all functions.

The exceptional null set may depend on the functions and, in CCC, on the slopes. No common full-measure set for every L∞L^\inftyL∞ function is required. No rate is supplied, and sparse or polynomial times are not directly covered.

A short consequence: does AAA cover 2n2n2n, 5n5n5n, and 9n9n9n?

Conditional on AAA Theorem 1.1, yes. Take d=9d=9d=9, place the three given functions at positions 2, 5, and 9, and use the constant 1 elsewhere. The average and limit become exactly the desired expressions. The same argument handles any finite set of distinct positive integer slopes. This is a formal consequence checked here, still conditional on the main theorem of AAA. It does not directly cover arbitrary-order slopes of mixed signs.

11. Why do fourfold averages lead to selected lengths and joint laws?

Triple oscillation controls at most three positions. The fourfold paper BBB seeks to turn pointwise failure into a joint law whose properties are incompatible. The following summarizes its construction.

Three structural notions

  • A factor retains part of a system's measurable information. For example, a map π:X→Y\pi :X\to Yπ:X→Y to a space YYY equipped with a measure-preserving transformation SSS satisfies π∘T=S∘π\pi \circ T=S\circ \piπ∘T=S∘π.
  • A joining is a probability measure on a product space with the prescribed coordinate marginals, invariant under the specified product action. Its coordinates may correlate, so a joining need not be a product measure.
  • A measure-distal system is built from the trivial factor through relatively compact extensions and inverse limits. It retains compact structure. Its precise definition uses invariant finitely generated modules in L2L^{2}L2, rather than an informal absence of disorder.
Relative compactness and relative weak mixing

“Relative” means a factor B\mathcal{B}B is retained as the base. Relative compactness requires L2L^{2}L2 to be densely spanned by invariant finitely generated L∞(B)L^\infty(\mathcal{B})L∞(B)-modules. Such a module consists of sums ∑jajFj\sum _{j}a_{j}F_{j}∑j​aj​Fj​, where FjF_{j}Fj​ are finitely many fixed L2L^{2}L2 functions and aja_{j}aj​ are bounded B\mathcal{B}B-measurable coefficients. Composition with TTT and T−1T^{-1}T−1 preserves the module. This expresses compact structure remaining over the given base.

Relative weak mixing requires, for bounded f,gf,gf,g with E(f∣B)=0\mathbb{E}(f\mid \mathcal{B})=0E(f∣B)=0, the condition 1H∑h=1H∥E[f⋅(g∘Th)∣B]∥22⟶0\frac{1}{H}\sum_{h=1}^{H}\left\Vert\mathbb{E}\bigl[f\cdot(g\circ T^h)\mid\mathcal{B}\bigr]\right\Vert_2^2\longrightarrow0H1​∑h=1H​​E[f⋅(g∘Th)∣B]​22​⟶0. Existence of the maximal factor, relative disjointness, nonergodic decomposition, and distal pointwise convergence are cited here as structural theorems.

Huang–Shao–Ye Theorem C proves pointwise convergence at every finite order for ergodic measure-distal systems. B/A additionally use a nonergodic version, maximal distal factors, and relative weak-mixing structure.

How does failure produce a positive correlation?

Split complex functions into real and imaginary parts, then split the real functions into means and mean-zero parts. The multilinear expansion is finite. If the fourfold almost-everywhere product limit fails, the results for at most three factors leave four real mean-zero functions whose product average still deviates from zero at arbitrarily late lengths on a positive-measure set. Restrict that set further and, if needed, reverse one function's sign so the deviations are positive. At each starting point xxx in the set, choose a sufficiently late length at which the average exceeds δ\deltaδ, and denote it by N(x)N(x)N(x). Use a predetermined length elsewhere. The candidates are countable, and choosing the first admissible one in a fixed order makes NNN measurable.

Average these selected orbit segments together and take a limiting law. It retains a nonzero correlation witnessing failure. But N(x)N(x)N(x) was chosen after observing xxx, so the joint law can change. Ordinary fixed-length L2L^{2}L2 convergence does not directly control all such selections.

12. Arbitrary finite order involves more than adding another factor

Paper AAA has ddd positions but still only three auxiliary groups. Among nonzero centered correlations it first minimizes support size kkk, then, at that fixed kkk, maximizes the number of output positions rrr. The order matters because the two choices serve different purposes.

  • Minimal kkk rules out smaller-support correlations and supplies product laws for specified spatial lists. It does not make arbitrary collections of complete states independent.
  • Maximal rrr is contradicted by a regrouping that keeps kkk fixed while increasing rrr.

A position may read the original spatial coordinate together with a proper subset of the three groups. This flexibility permits regrouping. A §§5–6, pp.14–21, carry out the argument; Appendix A, pp.21–24, derives the required tensor estimate.
