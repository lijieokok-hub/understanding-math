Unit 3, continued: Local detection, projection gain, and stopping intervals

This part studies the first part of D §§5.1–5.2, pp.27–29, principally Lemma 5.1 and its first application. A large local correlation is detected by a new bounded function. Adding a new private coordinate then increases projection energy by a fixed amount. The varying-function row estimate bounds how often this can happen along a spatial path.

We assume the precise local detection statement of H Lemma 4.12. What follows is the deduction of projection gain, the length bound for stopping intervals, and the sum over finitely many restarts. The inverse theorem and detection proof in H are not proved here.

7. First application: every retained interval passes its accuracy test

Source: the construction following Lemma 5.1, D p.29.

7.1 Complete the partition before inserting columns

Each construction starts with empty projections on its root RRR. At a spatial depth, first partition the whole root to that depth and recompute the local projections of retained columns on every cell. Next add the columns prescribed at that depth, and finally perform simultaneous insertion rounds. Intervals without requests keep their projections.

The next depth again refines the entire partition, including branches not tested again in this construction. Refinements, column additions, and identity transitions all remain in one finite nested projection sequence. This full sequence is needed for the joint operator bound.

7.2 Row conditions after a failed accuracy test

An original candidate interval III has depth s=s(I)s=s(I)s=s(I). Test

r=fj,s−(PFj,s)phys. r=f_{j,s}-(PF_{j,s})_{\mathrm{phys}}. r=fj,s​−(PFj,s​)phys​.

If dj,I(r)>e−kd_{j,I}(r)>\mathrm e^{-k}dj,I​(r)>e−k, (Detection) supplies a new ϕ\phiϕ with the common lower bound ρ\rhoρ in (Gain). Each request is assigned its projection-increment row on III, so no responsible row index is repeated.

At one depth, use the full depth-sss partition for both supports and observations. Every insertion round uses Fj,sF_{j,s}Fj,s​. Repeated rounds at the same depth are allowed. At the next depth the partition refines the previous one and indices remain nondecreasing. Split each increment over whole partition cells; the orthogonality argument in Corollary 4.5 preserves Bessel constant one.

Each path receives at most TTT bounded columns with private coefficient one, yielding a constant satisfying (Completed-size). Lemma 5.1 bounds the total length of first over-limit intervals by ∣R∣/4|R|/4∣R∣/4.

7.3 Restarting on the stopping intervals

Take each first over-limit interval as a new root. Transfer only the original summation intervals and descendants belonging to it in the original request tree. Restriction of the original function sequence retains the normalized block-variation condition. Begin a new empty-projection construction; detection, gain, and row estimates apply again, so its stopping intervals occupy at most one quarter of its length.

A new root cannot immediately stop at itself: that would make the stopped length equal the root length, contradicting the one-quarter bound. Every new root is therefore a strict descendant in the original finite request tree. Recursion terminates after finitely many steps.

The total root length at generation lll is at most 4−l∣R∣4^{-l}|R|4−l∣R∣. Hence all attempted roots satisfy

∑R′ a construction root∣R′∣≤∑l≥04−l∣R∣=43∣R∣.(Root-sum) \sum_{R'\text{ a construction root}}|R'| \le\sum_{l\ge0}4^{-l}|R| =\frac43|R|. \tag{Root-sum} R′ a construction root∑​∣R′∣≤l≥0∑​4−l∣R∣=34​∣R∣.(Root-sum)

Every original summation interval is finally assigned to exactly one construction that retains it. Projections needed by old ancestor summands continue through inheritance and local reprojection. New constructions may use different column families. Auxiliary partition cells do not thereby become new summands.
