Replacement and active patternsD Proposition 5.6 · (5.29)–(5.31)

Unit 3, continued: smallness of the full quadruple and the three main patterns

This part develops D Proposition 5.6, pp.40–42, equations (5.29)–(5.31). The preceding part constructed the full quadruple YYY and proved small form norms for every pair and for the entire local form. We now split YYY into a current-projection part BBB and a delayed increment UUU, handle the term with two UUU factors, and restrict the remaining three patterns to one family of active intervals.

We assume the precise statement of H Lemma 5.7: three fixed structured functions have only a controlled number of active scales along a path. Its proof using frequencies and rational heights is not developed here.

Place each of the four functions in its original position.

The source asserts that, for 0<δ≤10<\delta\le10<δ≤1, along any fixed spatial path,

#{I:act⁡I(ϕ)>δ}≤poly⁡(log⁡Dbase+log⁡(1/δ)), \#\{I:\operatorname{act}_I(\boldsymbol\phi)>\delta\} \le\operatorname{poly}\bigl(\log D_{\mathrm{base}}+\log(1/\delta)\bigr), #{I:actI​(ϕ)>δ}≤poly(logDbase​+log(1/δ)),

and

∑I on this pathact⁡I(ϕ)≤δact⁡I(ϕ)≤δpoly⁡(log⁡Dbase,log⁡(1/δ)).(Small-activity-sum) \sum_{\substack{I\text{ on this path}\\ \operatorname{act}_I(\boldsymbol\phi)\le\delta}} \operatorname{act}_I(\boldsymbol\phi) \le\delta\operatorname{poly}\bigl(\log D_{\mathrm{base}},\log(1/\delta)\bigr). \tag{Small-activity-sum} I on this pathactI​(ϕ)≤δ​∑​actI​(ϕ)≤δpoly(logDbase​,log(1/δ)).(Small-activity-sum)

The first bound counts large correlations; the second sums all small correlations. A counting bound at the single threshold δ\deltaδ is not enough. Group the positive small correlations into

Zero correlations contribute nothing. Thus the second bound follows by summing the counting estimates at all smaller thresholds; it is not a logically independent additional assumption.

8. Deriving a truncated pattern sum from smallness of the full YYY

The preceding part also gives the intervalwise bound for the complete quadruple

∣HI(YI)∣≤η. |\mathcal H_I(Y_I)|\le\eta. ∣HI​(YI​)∣≤η.

Equation (Four-patterns) gives a triangle inequality as well. Combining them,

∣HI(YI)∣≤min⁡{η, ∣HI(zIUU)∣+∑z∈P∣HI(zI)∣}≤∣HI(zIUU)∣+min⁡{η,∑z∈P∣HI(zI)∣}≤∣HI(zIUU)∣+∑z∈Pmin⁡{η,∣HI(zI)∣}.(Truncation) \begin{aligned} |\mathcal H_I(Y_I)| &\le\min\left\{\eta,\, |\mathcal H_I(z_I^{UU})| +\sum_{z\in\mathcal P}|\mathcal H_I(z_I)|\right\}\\ &\le|\mathcal H_I(z_I^{UU})| +\min\left\{\eta,\sum_{z\in\mathcal P}|\mathcal H_I(z_I)|\right\}\\ &\le|\mathcal H_I(z_I^{UU})| +\sum_{z\in\mathcal P}\min\{\eta,|\mathcal H_I(z_I)|\}. \end{aligned} \tag{Truncation} ∣HI​(YI​)∣​≤min{η,∣HI​(zIUU​)∣+z∈P∑​∣HI​(zI​)∣}≤∣HI​(zIUU​)∣+min{η,z∈P∑​∣HI​(zI​)∣}≤∣HI​(zIUU​)∣+z∈P∑​min{η,∣HI​(zI​)∣}.​(Truncation)

The last two steps use only the following inequalities for nonnegative numbers:

min⁡(η,A+B)≤B+min⁡(η,A),min⁡(η,∑jaj)≤∑jmin⁡(η,aj). \min(\eta,A+B)\le B+\min(\eta,A), \qquad \min\left(\eta,\sum_j a_j\right)\le\sum_j\min(\eta,a_j). min(η,A+B)≤B+min(η,A),min(η,j∑​aj​)≤j∑​min(η,aj​).

When ∑jaj≤η\sum_j a_j\le\eta∑j​aj​≤η, the two sides of the second inequality are equal. When the sum exceeds η\etaη, the right-hand side is at least η\etaη, so the inequality still holds.

Define the remaining quantity to be estimated for each pattern by

MR(z)=∑I∈AR∣I∣min⁡{η,∣HI(zI)∣}. M_R(z)=\sum_{I\in\mathcal A_R}|I| \min\{\eta,|\mathcal H_I(z_I)|\}. MR​(z)=I∈AR​∑​∣I∣min{η,∣HI​(zI​)∣}.

Sum (Truncation) over the assigned original intervals. Apply (Two-U) to the double-increment term and (Inactive) to the inactive terms. This gives D equation (5.31):

∑I∈IR∣I∣ ∣HI(YI)∣≤CηΛk2∣R∣+e−k∣R∣+∑z∈PMR(z).(Active-reduction) \sum_{I\in\mathcal I_R}|I|\,|\mathcal H_I(Y_I)| \le C\eta\Lambda_k^2|R| +\mathrm e^{-k}|R| +\sum_{z\in\mathcal P}M_R(z). \tag{Active-reduction} I∈IR​∑​∣I∣∣HI​(YI​)∣≤CηΛk2​∣R∣+e−k∣R∣+z∈P∑​MR​(z).(Active-reduction)

This deduction does not bound an individual pattern HI(zI)\mathcal H_I(z_I)HI​(zI​) by η\etaη, nor does it give arbitrary pairs in that pattern the small form norms of the full YYY. Individual patterns can be large. The subsequent analysis must control exactly MR(z)M_R(z)MR​(z), with its minimum retained.

10. The data that must be retained for localization

D §6 uses the complete functions B,UB,UB,U, the same original active interval family, and the associated small pairwise bounds for the full YYY. The two low positions were expanded into scalar columns only for the preceding high-position replacement. Three underlying columns were expanded only for the inactive-term estimate here. No additional small correlation for a separated pattern can be carried into the next stage without justification.

On any new normalized subroot, restriction preserves the normalization of the sequence, the private coordinates of the underlying columns, and inheritance. Reprove the delayed-increment square-energy bound on that subroot using the full row estimate before repeating this argument.

The preceding replacement error for X→YX\to YX→Y is a separate global estimate summed over all roots. By contrast, (Active-reduction) is proved anew on one fixed root. The global error cannot be rewritten as a proportional error on each individual root without proof.
