Technical appendix: an all-scale prediction catalog for two fixed low columns

Starting from the chart expansions on pp. 18–22 of H, this appendix proves the finite prediction catalog needed for Lemma 5.1 and Corollary 5.2 on pp. 29–32. The essential order is to fix the unscaled labels first and then prove an absolute scale sum. A uniform approximation at each scale is not enough. We use the October 5, 2026 version; all page numbers below refer to that version.

Fourier analysis first treats fixed inputs and square-energy sequences. Projection inputs also require the full-depth estimate in the spatial compression appendix. Rational splitting and finite expansion use the analytic results in the technical appendix to Chapter 10. We do not assume the conclusions of Lemma 5.1 or Corollary 5.2, or use the later basic-catalog or active-cell results.

1. The target and its inputs

We use the following inputs.

  • The kernel assumptions on p. 4 of H: interior support, vanishing single-slot marginals in all four slots, and normalized derivative bounds.
  • Rational splitting comes from Lemma 4.3, pp. 16–18 of H, and finite expansion on a fixed box from Lemma 4.4, pp. 18–20.   • Lemma 3.4, pp. 9–13 of H, is used only for projection sequences. Its proof is in the spatial compression appendix. It fixes the column system before proving the full-depth estimate and does not follow from the prediction catalog.

5. How bounded height controls scales without a catalog-size cost

Source: Lemma 3.5 on pp. 13–14 of H. We explain its elementary basis and the form used here.

8. Conditions to retain throughout the proof

8.1 Five points about all-scale summation

H, p. 19 · H, p. 20 · H, p. 30 · H, p. 31

  • Finite pooling of projections and tail envelopes in Lemma 4.4 follows directly if repeated labels are retained.
  • For the absolute scale sum of a fixed tail phase, first prove fixed-modulation Bessel and then use the quadratic geometric tail. The order is noncircular.
  • To use height bins in Bessel, temporarily add the real parameter ξ\xiξ for each Fourier variable. This increases the dimension by only 1. Do not add a separate Bessel constant for every frequency.
  • At arbitrarily fine depths, the zero quadratic phase does not supply a summable absolute-error factor. The linear part is instead controlled by mean-zero Bessel, so no depth cutoff is needed.
  • Small divisors near resonance only shift the locations of scale intervals. They do not increase the number of occupied magnitude bins beyond a polynomial.

8.2 The order of the steps

H, p. 19 · H, p. 20 · H, p. 30 · H, p. 31 · H, p. 32

  • The fixed-linear-frequency Bessel estimate needed on pp. 30–31 is proved first in Section 4 of this appendix. The tail estimate therefore does not use the multifrequency conclusion still to be proved.
  • The cutoffs, periods, and lattice labels on pp. 19–20 are chosen uniformly under one height budget, and λ,γ\lambda,\gammaλ,γ are unscaled parameters.
  • The per-depth contraction estimates on p. 30 are applied separately to fixed inputs, projection inputs, and energy sequences. When energy parameters occur in both slots, an L² factor is allowed and absorbed into a fixed-degree polynomial.
  • The fixed multiplier on p. 31 is applied by precomposing the Bessel row family. The bump after multiplication need not have mean zero.
  • The truncations on p. 32 are ordered as follows: first separation shells, then small ArA_rAr​ bands, and finally the pooled Fourier tail. The final accuracy cannot be fixed before allowing for the preceding coefficient budgets.
