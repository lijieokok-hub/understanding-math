6.3 Why neither standardness nor mixing is required

The argument only samples measurable functions along finite orbit segments, applies an integer-sequence inequality, and integrates using measure preservation. It uses no conditional distributions, ergodic decomposition, or topological model, so the probability space need not be standard. No decay of correlations appears, so mixing is not required either.

Invertibility gives meaning to the negative times in the integer sequences. If the functions are initially specified only as L∞L^\inftyL∞ equivalence classes, choose bounded representatives and remove the null set obtained from all integer translates of their exceptional sets, as in Unit 1.

7. Constants and the order of limits

After fixing the slopes and one averaging weight, first obtain D,C0D,C_0D,C0​ from Lemma 3.2, then determine ρ,Aρ\rho,A_\rhoρ,Aρ​ from the integer reconstruction coefficients. None of these quantities depends on the later scale endpoints or input functions.

For each specified sequence of endpoints, construct the scale-varying test functions and choose the orbit-truncation margin QQQ. Only then let the orbit length MMM tend to infinity. The constant in (T) must be uniform in depth and block counts from the outset; otherwise the argument cannot yield (O) from Unit 1.

Together, the first two units establish a conditional deduction: the local absolute-sum estimate in C Theorem 2.2 implies the triple pointwise conclusion for mixing systems in C Theorem 1.1.
