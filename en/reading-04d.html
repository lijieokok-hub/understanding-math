Unit 4, continued: Preserving the required independence of three auxiliary groups

This part studies Definition 5.1, Lemma 5.2, Proposition 5.3, and Corollary 5.4 in B §5, pp.14–18. The preceding parts established the selected law, its conditional parameter laws, and a finite tensor-projection estimate. We now use them to ask which independence relations survive when the averaging length depends on the starting point.

We additionally assume mixing of every finite order, as stated in B (2.1). The paper imports this assertion from R, Rokhlin's multiple-mixing problem for one transformation. A step-by-step reconstruction of its main proof is given in the companion reading on multiple mixing. It remains an input to this article; the definition of ordinary mixing does not supply it.

2. The same product laws hold given the maximal distal factor

Source: B Lemma 5.2, pp.14–15.

2.1 Fixing the phases at an integer time

The phase factor VVV is a finite-dimensional torus translation with its actual joint phase distribution. It is therefore compact and contained in WYW_YWY​. 3.1 Reduction to centered spatial functions

Test the required laws with bounded spatial products and continuous functions of the new phases. The phase space is a finite-dimensional torus. Finite sums of products of one-coordinate functions uniformly approximate its continuous functions, so the product-test formula for the selected law applies.
