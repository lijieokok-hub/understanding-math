One shared random coloring and a small exceptional set

This chapter studies Lemma 6.3 of H, in Section 6.2, pages 41–43. It supplies a fixed successful color realization for the historical ball masks in the following Section 6.3. The source is the fixed October 5, 2026 version of An L3L^3L3 bound for the trilinear Hilbert transform.

We prove two statements. For every realization of the delay arrays, each vertex changes color only a controlled number of times. Moreover, one global realization can be chosen so that the first cells with failed markings have any prescribed small fixed total length relative to the root. This second statement uses one realization for the whole tree, rather than a separate choice of random numbers for each spatial path.

The inputs are fixed original vertex lists and graph distances that do not increase with depth. The main columns, helper columns, and linearized modes constructed later do not add graph vertices, as stated explicitly on page 39 of the source.

1. Fix the finite tree, vertices, and graphs before sampling

Fix a finite dyadic tree below a root interval RRR. At each cell III, write V(I)\mathcal V(I)V(I) for the original vertex list, consisting of the occurrences of original columns in all relevant slots. A child inherits its parent's list and may append new vertices. The admission order, including the order of simultaneous births, is fixed in advance. Different branches may admit different vertices. Two vertices with the same curvature tag are still distinct occurrences of original columns, and their delays are sampled independently.

Assume that the total number of vertices ever admitted on each downward path from the root is at most the integer D≥2D\ge2D≥2. The total number of vertices in the whole tree need not be at most DDD. It is finite because the tree is finite, but it will not enter the final error bound.

The graph is defined on all hypothetical curvature tags satisfying the basic bounds. At absolute depth sss, write dsd_sds​ for its distance, which may equal +∞+\infty+∞. Equal tags have distance zero, and

ds+1(v,w)≤ds(v,w).(Graph monotonicity) d_{s+1}(v,w)\le d_s(v,w). \tag{Graph monotonicity} ds+1​(v,w)≤ds​(v,w).(Graph monotonicity)

These distances depend on the curvature tags and absolute depth. They do not depend on spatial location, whether a vertex is currently present, colors, or delay arrays. Distances are defined even for tags whose vertices have not yet been born.

The order of quantifiers is therefore as follows: first fix the lists, admission data, and entire graph family; then sample the arrays. The conclusions are uniform over all such fixed graph families. They do not require one array to work simultaneously for every possible graph family or every edge threshold.

6. Monotone scores allow only finitely many successful stages

We first isolate the counting argument. Fix one target vertex and one round. Competitors are added over time, every existing competitor's score is nondecreasing, and the top score always belongs to {0,…,d0}\{0,\ldots,d_0\}{0,…,d0​}.

Consider a consecutive time interval with constant top score ggg. Suppose a successful marking occurs within it, with winner zzz. At each earlier time in the same interval, some competitor already has score ggg. Its score cannot decrease and is still ggg at the successful marking, so it must be the unique winner zzz. Every other competitor's earlier score is no greater than its score at the successful marking, and is therefore at most g−5g-5g−5.

It follows that, within an interval of constant top score, the successful times form an initial consecutive interval with a fixed winner. Once a rival destroys the gap, success cannot return while the top score remains unchanged. Each round has at most d0+1d_0+1d0​+1 successful stages.

Describe the state of a round as either failure or success with its top score and winner. This state changes at at most 2(d0+1)2(d_0+1)2(d0​+1) boundaries. Taking all boundaries from d0d_0d0​ rounds, the number of stages for the first successful round and its top score and winner is at most

2d0(d0+1)+1≤5d02.(Round-selection stages) 2d_0(d_0+1)+1\le5d_0^2. \tag{Round-selection stages} 2d0​(d0​+1)+1≤5d02​.(Round-selection stages)

No independence of the states over time is assumed. This is a deterministic count for every fixed realization of the delays.

The primary rounds satisfy these assumptions because graph distances are nonincreasing with depth and births only add competitors. Within a fixed context and an interval with fixed lll, the high rounds satisfy them as well: existing scores are constant, and only new competitors are added.

9. A fixed score situation has a uniform success probability

A fixed score situation consists of a finite set of at most DDD competitors and a fixed penalty cz∈[0,∞]c_z\in[0,\infty]cz​∈[0,∞] for each competitor. At least one penalty is zero. The scores are Xz−czX_z-c_zXz​−cz​, where the XzX_zXz​ are independent untruncated geometric variables. In the primary and high rounds, the penalties are the integer parts of the corresponding distance quotients.

For each outcome of the geometric variables, use a fixed order to choose one top-score winner zzz, and increase only XzX_zXz​ by 555. In the resulting outcome, this winner is unique and leads every other competitor by at least 555.

This map is injective: the unique winner in the image identifies the coordinate that was increased, and subtracting 555 recovers the original outcome. Since

P(Xz=n)=2−n−1,n=0,1,…, \mathbb P(X_z=n)=2^{-n-1},\quad n=0,1,\ldots, P(Xz​=n)=2−n−1,n=0,1,…,

13. Integrate over space to select one global realization

There are finitely many births in the whole tree, and each truncated delay has finitely many values. The entire sample space is therefore finite. All colors and failure events can be defined simultaneously on every original cell. The suprema over integer depths in the frozen profiles are also well-defined measurable functions.

14. Why use the first failure cells?

Under ω∗\omega_*ω∗​, send the first cell on each path where a required marking fails to a fresh attempt. Let F\mathcal FF be the collection of these first failure cells. Include each cell only once. A cell with an already failed ancestor is not included as a first failure cell.

Two intersecting dyadic cells are nested, whereas no first failure cell can be a strict ancestor of another. The first failure cells are therefore pairwise disjoint. Each also lies in B(ω∗)\mathcal B(\omega_*)B(ω∗​), so

With half-open dyadic cells, disjointness holds literally. Using closed intervals changes only a null set of boundary points.

If the construction requires markings only at some cells or vertices, its first required failure cells correspond to a subset of the failure events already counted and satisfy the same bound. On the retained paths, every actual use of a color or historical mask has successful primary and high markings.

This chapter controls this source of failure only. Combining it with the other catalog, size, and maximal-function stopping rules into the full restart argument of Section 10 of H still requires the respective error and root-length bounds.

15. What is supplied to the historical masks, and what remains

We can now fix the chosen ω∗\omega_*ω∗​. Each vertex's primary delays remain unchanged along inherited lists; the high arrays are shared by all frozen contexts; colors are nonincreasing; and each successful group has fixed primary-round, score, cohort, ball-level, count, high-round, and winner data. After removing the first failure roots, this chapter supplies the successful marking realization assumed in the chapter on historical ball masks.

This conclusion relies on the data being fixed in advance as in Section 1. The original lists and graph family cannot be altered arbitrarily after observing the delays. New main columns, helper columns, and modes cannot be admitted as new graph vertices. Allowing such adaptive changes would invalidate the deterministic situation list in Section 10 and the situation list depending only on the primary arrays in Section 11.

This chapter does not develop the same-tag template tests, catalog closure for changing projection histories, or the full two-sided estimate. Those arguments may use the color realization constructed here, but they do not follow automatically from the small probabilistic exceptional set.
