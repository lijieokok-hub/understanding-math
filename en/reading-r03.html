Chapter 3. Operator squares and completeness of exact lines

An exact line is a one-dimensional space spanned by a function of modulus one that can be multiplied by modulus-one functions at the other positions in the same row or column to give a constant phase.

This chapter follows Definition 4.1 and Lemmas 4.2–4.7 of R (pp. 14–21). Fix a parameter square satisfying the local kernel identities for grouped rectangles from Chapter 2. The argument decomposes the space visible from both the row and the column into exact lines. The steps establish intertwining of projections, fixed positive parts, an abelian unitary group, and the reverse inclusion needed for completeness.

1. The conclusion to be proved

Fix a full parameter square EEE satisfying Proposition 3.5. All subsequent probability laws are conditioned on this EEE. Partition the rows into three nonempty groups P,A,BP,A,BP,A,B and the columns into three nonempty groups r,s,tr,s,tr,s,t. A rectangular cell Yi,lY_{i,l}Yi,l​ is the complete state vector in that rectangle.

Set Hi,l=L2(μi,l)H_{i,l}=L^{2}(\mu _{i,l})Hi,l​=L2(μi,l​), where μi,l\mu _{i,l}μi,l​ is the marginal law of the rectangular cell. The inner product is linear in the second variable:

⟨f,g⟩=∫f‾ g dμ. \langle f,g\rangle=\int\overline f\,g\,\mathrm d\mu. ⟨f,g⟩=∫f​gdμ.

Every span below means a closed linear span. The row-active space VVV of a cell is the closed span of the conditional expectations, onto that cell, of bounded functions of the other two cells in its row. The column-active space WWW is defined using the other two cells in its column. Both contain the constants and are invariant under complex conjugation.

The aim is to show that D=V∩WD=V\cap WD=V∩W is spanned by modulus-one functions satisfying both the row and the column product relations. At the corner cell (P,r)(P,r)(P,r), the proof gives the more specific description

DP,r=L2(σ(h(z))),z=YP,r.(34) D_{P,r}=L^2(\sigma(h(z))),\quad z=Y_{P,r}.\tag{34} DP,r​=L2(σ(h(z))),z=YP,r​.(34)

Here, h(z)h(z)h(z) takes values in an abelian unitary group constructed in the proof. Finite monomials in the group's characters, evaluated at h(z)h(z)h(z), satisfy both exact product relations and span this L2L^{2}L2 space.

Completeness is the remaining issue: constructing exact lines gives only L2(σ(h))⊆DL^{2}(\sigma (h))\subseteq DL2(σ(h))⊆D. The reverse inclusion needs a separate argument; DDD cannot yet be treated as an algebra of functions.

  1. The three rectangular cells in each grouped row or column are pairwise independent.
  2. Given the complete grouped row PPP, any two grouped columns are independent, and each column's conditional law depends only on its entries in PPP.

3. Two basic facts about exact tuples

Definition 4.1 and Lemma 4.2 appear on pp. 14–15. For an mmm-coordinate law λ\lambdaλ, an exact tuple satisfies ∣χi∣=1|\chi _i|=1∣χi​∣=1 and ∏χi(Yi)=c\prod \chi _i(Y_i)=c∏χi​(Yi​)=c, where ccc is a fixed constant of modulus one. Second, if m≥3m\ge 3m≥3 and every subfamily of m−1m-1m−1 coordinates is independent, distinct exact lines are orthogonal and their partner lines are unique. The partners are the lines at the other coordinates that form an exact tuple with the given line. To prove this, put ai=∣∫χi dμi∣a_i=|\int \chi _i \,\mathrm{d}\mu _i|ai​=∣∫χi​dμi​∣. If some ai=0a_i=0ai​=0, the product identity forces at least one other aj=0a_j=0aj​=0. Every right-hand side then contains a zero, so all aja_jaj​ vanish. If they are all positive, set bi=−log⁡ai≥0b_i=-\log a_i\ge 0bi​=−logai​≥0. Then 2bi=∑jbj2b_i=\sum _jb_j2bi​=∑j​bj​, and summing gives (m−2)∑jbj=0(m-2)\sum _jb_j=0(m−2)∑j​bj​=0. Thus all ai=1a_i=1ai​=1. The conditions ∣χi∣=1|\chi _i|=1∣χi​∣=1 and ∣E[χi]∣=1|\mathbb E[\chi_i]|=1∣E[χi​]∣=1 imply that χi\chi _iχi​ is almost everywhere constant. An exact tuple therefore consists either entirely of constants or entirely of mean-zero components.

The coordinatewise quotient of two exact tuples is again exact. If the two lines differ at one cell, the quotient there is nonconstant. All quotient functions then have mean zero, so corresponding representatives are orthogonal at every cell. If the lines agree at one cell, that quotient is constant, forcing every quotient to be constant; this proves uniqueness of the partner lines. Multiplication and conjugation give the group operations. A separable Hilbert space contains at most countably many nonzero mutually orthogonal lines.

The completeness proof below mainly uses the inclusion of exact lines in active spaces and invariance under conjugation. Uniqueness of partners does not replace the operator argument.
