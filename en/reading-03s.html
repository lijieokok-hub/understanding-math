Unit 3, supplement: closure of main projections and localization

This supplement develops D §6.1, pp.42–44, equations (6.1)–(6.5). In the source order it precedes variation of localized outputs. It defines the groups, masks, and main projections used later, and is where depth-varying functions first enter H's localization argument.

H's graph-mask construction, different-label separation, and same-label detection remain explicit inputs. We develop finite projection closure, Bessel synthesis on whole endpoint intervals, and the binary search that places the four functions on an original cell in one current group.

1. Labels, groups, and local orthogonal projections

1.1 Graph vertices and new columns

An actual occurrence of a basic column corresponds to a graph vertex. Each vertex carries H's full normalized curvature label, including horizontal-coordinate data. In position lll, the quadratic phase is the label phase multiplied by

κl,(κ0,κ1,κ2,κ3)=(−1,3,−3,1). \kappa_l,\qquad (\kappa_0,\kappa_1,\kappa_2,\kappa_3)=(-1,3,-3,1). κl​,(κ0​,κ1​,κ2​,κ3​)=(−1,3,−3,1).

Linear modulation is not part of the curvature label.

Graph distance depends on the labels and absolute depth from the original JJJ, and is nonincreasing with depth. Later main or auxiliary columns are attached to existing vertices with the same labels. Adding columns creates no new vertices.

1.2 Groups and masks

A group GGG has a fixed color and a collection of actual center uses. Its mask with enlargement hhh,

MG,h(I) \mathcal M_{G,h}(I) MG,h​(I)

is the set of vertices permitted on interval III. It records earlier center uses, so it may continue to be inherited after the group ceases to be current on a branch.

For rank rrr, define

as the orthogonal projection in L2(I;H)L^2(I;\mathcal H)L2(I;H) onto the span of existing columns with rank at most rrr and labels in M\mathcal MM, using coefficients constant on the whole of III. Coefficients may depend on III but cannot vary arbitrarily pointwise within one III.

For fixed III, enlarging a mask or adding columns increases the projection range. Each individual mask projection is also inherited along complete depth partitions. Required continuations must be defined even on intervals without actual use.

A positive averaging filter averages PPP expanded projections:

Q=1P∑p=1PPp. Q=\frac1P\sum_{p=1}^P P_p. Q=P1​p=1∑P​Pp​.

The operator QQQ is a positive contraction, generally not an orthogonal projection. A history operator is a prescribed ordered product of these filters and their complementary operators 1−Q1-Q1−Q. Fixing individual projection choices in each average and expanding complements gives finitely many ordered orthogonal-projection products. Projections in different positions need not commute.

1.3 Usable endpoints

An endpoint interval UUU is usable for a group mask when the mask is nonempty and UUU or a descendant has an actual use of that group. The endpoint need not itself be a current center use.

H Lemma 6.4 gives three geometric properties:

  1. If the actual center is vvv, every existing vertex whose distance from vvv is at most hhh lies in the level-hhh mask.
  2. Graph neighbors of vertices in the level-hhh mask lie in the level-h+1h+1h+1 mask.
  3. For different groups of the same color on overlapping usable endpoints, their mask vertices have distance greater than one at the finer endpoint depth.

These are the geometric inputs for localization and synthesis across groups. We do not reconstruct the colors, center competition, and historical masks needed to prove them.

4.4 The order of parameter choices is not circular

The count uses the number of named contexts, template complexity, and history length. It does not use the numerical graph-edge height HedH_{\rm ed}Hed​ or the later mode-approximation precision. Thus first determine
