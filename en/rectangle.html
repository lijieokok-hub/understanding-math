A story in geometry · 01

Four corners
and a twist

Draw a loop without crossing your own line. Can four points on it be the corners of a rectangle?

Yes, always. Yet ask for a square, and the same question remains open for completely general loops.

01 / One continuous loop

The word “some” carries a lot of weight

Imagine reshaping a circle into a potato, a teardrop, or something far less regular. As long as the line is continuous, closes up and never crosses itself, it is a Jordan curve: the image of a continuous injection from the circle \(S^1\) into the plane. “Inscribed” means the four vertices lie on the curve; the sides need not lie inside its enclosed region.

There are two different challenges. May the curve choose the rectangle's shape for us, or must it accept a shape specified in advance? Their answers part ways.

SOLVED

Some rectangle

Every plane Jordan curve contains the four vertices of a nondegenerate rectangle. Vaughan's proof appeared in Meyerson's article. [1]

OPEN IN GENERAL

A prescribed shape

Does every Jordan curve inscribe every specified rectangular shape, including a square? This remains open in general, although the smooth case has been solved. [2]

02 / Make a prediction

What do the diagonals know?

The diagonals of a rectangle share a midpoint and have equal length. Conversely, two distinct nonzero segments with the same midpoint and length are the diagonals of a nondegenerate rectangle.

In a circle, any two distinct diameters have these properties automatically. Move one of them. When is the resulting rectangle a square? How does its shape change?

Why do those two diagonal conditions suffice?

Write the endpoints as \(m\pm u\) and \(m\pm v\), where \(|u|=|v|>0\) and \(u\ne\pm v\). The four points \(m+u,m+v,m-u,m-v\) are distinct, and adjacent side vectors satisfy

\[(v-u)\cdot(-u-v)=|u|^2-|v|^2=0.\]

Thus all four angles are right angles. In the experiment, equal length and midpoint were built in; for an arbitrary curve, finding two such chords is the actual problem.

03 / A change of viewpoint

Give every chord an address

Searching directly for four corners is a lot to keep track of. Vaughan's move is to regard a pair of points \(\{a,b\}\) as a single chord and assign it three coordinates: the two coordinates of its midpoint, followed by its length.

\[F(\{a,b\})=\left(\frac{a+b}{2},\ |a-b|\right)\in\mathbb R^2\times[0,\infty).\]

A rectangle appears when two different chords have the same address. The geometric question has become a question about whether this map must collide. [1, p. 71]

Experiment · unordered pairs

Swap the endpoints

\((A,B)\) and \((B,A)\) describe the same chord. Swap the labels: midpoint, length and address stay the same.

During the animation both endpoints travel around the ellipse. At the end they exchange places and the original chord returns. Pause at any time; with reduced motion the exchange happens instantly.

Order: A → B; unordered pair {A, B} is unchanged.

This illustrates the identification locally; proving that the entire pair space is a Möbius band takes a global topological argument.

The key assertion. For any plane Jordan curve, \(F\) must send two distinct unordered pairs to the same point. Here is the argument, with its topological inputs made explicit.

What space do all chords form?

Include zero-length chords \(\{a,a\}\). Since exchanging endpoints changes nothing, the parameter space is \((S^1\times S^1)/(s,t)\sim(t,s)\). A standard topological fact identifies it with a closed Möbius band; its boundary consists precisely of the repeated pairs \(\{a,a\}\).

As an intuition, let the two endpoints separate and travel around the circle until their roles exchange. Because the pair is unordered, the parameter strip closes with a half twist. The precise statement is the homeomorphism of the quotient space, not the picture alone.

Suppose there are no collisions

Assume \(F\) is injective. The band is compact and \(\mathbb R^3\) is Hausdorff, so this continuous injection is an embedding onto its image. Exactly the boundary pairs have length zero; they map to the original curve at height zero. The rest of the band lies strictly above the plane.

Cap the boundary

By the Jordan–Schoenflies theorem, the curve together with its enclosed region is a topological closed disk. Glue that disk, sitting at height zero, to the band's boundary. It meets the band only along the boundary. A Möbius band capped by a disk is the real projective plane \(\mathbb{RP}^2\).

A collision cannot be avoided

We would have embedded \(\mathbb{RP}^2\) in \(\mathbb R^3\), contradicting the theorem that it cannot be embedded there. Hence \(F\) has a collision. It cannot occur at zero height: \(F(\{a,a\})=(a,0)\). Nor can two distinct equal-midpoint chords share one endpoint, since the other endpoint would then also agree. Their four endpoints are distinct and form a rectangle.

Three standard results are used here rather than proved from the drawings: the identification of the pair space with a Möbius band, the Jordan–Schoenflies theorem, and the non-embeddability of \(\mathbb{RP}^2\) in three-space. See [1] and [3].

The argument does not locate a rectangle. It shows that a world in which every chord has a different address would force an impossible embedding.
04 / Beyond the proof

Why doesn't the same argument find a square?

Matching midpoint and length gives a rectangle. A square asks for one more condition: perpendicular diagonals. The topological collision supplies no control over their angle. “There exists a shape” and “every prescribed shape occurs” are different statements.

Approximating a rough curve by smooth ones is tempting, but a sequence of inscribed rectangles might shrink to a single point. Without a uniform nondegeneracy estimate, its limit need not be a rectangle.

1911 · The square peg question

Toeplitz posed the question of an inscribed square for every Jordan curve; Matschke's survey provides historical context. [3]

1981 · One rectangle is guaranteed

Meyerson published Vaughan's topological argument for all plane Jordan curves, without prescribing the aspect ratio. [1]

2021 · Every shape for smooth curves

Greene and Lobb proved that every smooth Jordan curve inscribes a rectangle in each prescribed similarity class: the aspect ratio is fixed in advance, while size and orientation are free. [2]

2026 · The boundary moves

A preprint by Asano and Ike establishes every prescribed shape for a large class including all rectifiable Jordan curves. Another preprint by Greene and Lobb shows that, for every Jordan curve, the set of attainable angles between diagonals has positive measure. Both are preprints; a positive-measure set need not contain \(90^\circ\). [4] [5]

As of 2 November 2026, the prescribed-shape problem for arbitrary Jordan curves, including the square case, remains open.
